## Q1. Naming and counting conventions (degrees, inversions, direction)

### Takeoff
The degree number is the total rotation, counted in 180° steps. Where a source spells it out, flip rotation and spin rotation are added together ("2 flips + 1 twist = 1080"; FIS: rodeo = backflip + FS 180). The prefix (double/triple/quad/quint) counts separate off-axis inversions. Direction prefixes are FS, BS, cab (= switch FS) and switch BS.

### Cited Findings
- FIS list: a doublecork is when the athlete "goes off-axis two separate times in the same trick". Examples: "Backside DubCork 1080, Frontside DubCork 1260". — [FIS Tricks List](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html) (snippet)
- FIS list: "720º rotation, 2.0 full rotations"; 900 = 2.5 rotations. Degrees are counted as full rotations. — [FIS Tricks List](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html) (snippet)
- FIS list: Rodeo = "A backflip with a frontside 180 on the y axis, opposed to the frontside cork 5 which is off axis". The rodeo 540 is therefore read as a 360° flip plus a 180° spin, and its name sums both. — [FIS Tricks List](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html) (snippet, as already quoted in note 12)
- Rodeo: "an invert done riding fakie or forward, rider rotating frontside or backside while flipping … can be done with a 540, 720, 900 and 1080". — FIS list / NBC glossary, via search summary ([FIS](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html); [NBC glossary](https://nbcolympics.com/post/220836))
- DC 1080 = "two head-over-heels flips with one full twist". — [NBC Sports explainer](https://www.nbcsportsbayarea.com/news/sports/beijing-winter-olympics/triple-cork-1080-chicken-salad-snowboarding-terms-explained/)
- Double Michalchuk 1080 ("Chuck Taylor", Taylor Gold): "a double backflip off the back wall with one horizontal spin mixed in". Two backflips (720) plus one spin (360) = 1080, the same summing convention. — [NBC Sports explainer](https://www.nbcsportsbayarea.com/news/sports/beijing-winter-olympics/triple-cork-1080-chicken-salad-snowboarding-terms-explained/) (snippet)
- Double McTwist 1260 (Shaun White, Vancouver 2010): "two flips while completing three and a half spins", "3 1/2 twists he crams into two head-over-heels flips". — [Olympics.com](https://olympics.com/en/news/shaun-white-signature-move-double-mctwist-cork); [ESPN](https://www.espn.com/top/story/_/id/4871427) (snippets)
- Forum comment on Parrot's quad underflip: some fans dispute the "1620" label for quad flips "since the spins are counted differently from the flip count" (informal opinion). — [Newschoolers forum](https://newschoolers.com/forum/thread/808291/Another-snowboarding-quad) (snippet)
- Cab = switch frontside. Switch backside has no nickname ("switch backside 1980", Olympics 2026). — see note 12-trick-fase-3; [Bleacher Report](https://bleacherreport.com/articles/25391747-kira-kimura-wins-gold-medal-snowboarding-big-air-olympics-2026)

### Inferences
- [I] The two ways of counting are numerically the same. Whether the name is read as "total revolutions × 360" or "flip degrees + spin degrees", a DC 1080 comes out at 3 × 360. Judges count what they see: the number of half-turns the rider's facing direction completes over the whole trick. For the game, D = total rotation angle about the angular-momentum axis, rounded to 180, and k is counted separately when the body's up-vector dips below the horizon. This matches the existing project rule in 12-trick-fase-3.
- [D] The landing stance depends only on D/180 being odd or even, whatever k is. 540/900/1260/1620/1980/2340 land in the opposite stance.

## Q2. The rule of thumb on inversions vs revolutions, and impossible or never-done combos

### Takeoff
Sourced tricks never break **k ≤ floor(D/360)** (k = inversions, D = degrees). The tighter "k = revolutions − 1" rule fits every standard multi-cork (DC 1080, TC 1440, QC 1800, QC 1980). It does not fit single corks: cork 540 has 1.5 revolutions and 1 inversion, cork 360 has 1 and 1. Flip-based families (McTwist, underflip, Michalchuk) reach the floor limit itself: the cab triple underflip 1260 has 3 inversions in 3.5 revolutions, and the quad underflip 1620 has 4 in 4.5.

### Cited Findings
- BS quad cork 1980 (Yuki Kadono, Hakuba 2017): "5 and a half rotations and 4 inversions". — [SnowBrains](https://snowbrains.com/video-worlds-first-1980-quad-cork-snowboard/) (snippet)
- BS 2160 (Ogiwara, The Nines, April 2022) is described by X Games as "the first to land a 2160 (and … the first to land a quintuple cork)", so 6 revolutions and 5 inversions. Note 12 already flagged this count as contested. — search summary of [X Games athlete page](https://www.xgames.com/athletes/hiroto-ogiwara/)
- BS 2340 (Ogiwara, X Games Aspen Jan 2025, 97.33): "six and a half rotations", mute grab per Yardbarker. **No source states its inversion count.** — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/01/26/nx-s1-5275434/snowboard-ski-2340-x-games); [Yardbarker](https://www.yardbarker.com/general_sports/articles/x_games_2025_world_records_broken_with_2340_mute_grab_and_backside_triple_cork_1440/s1_17436_41642634) (snippets)
- FS 2160 (Ian Matteoli, Stubai, after a 2023 injury) is the first frontside 2160. Inversion count not given. — [Teton Gravity](https://www.tetongravity.com/worlds-first-2160-landed-on-a-snowboard); [Red Bull athlete page](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/athlete/ian-matteoli) (snippets)
- Quad cork 1800: four off-axis flips, five rotations (Morgan 2015, Kleveland first in competition 2017). — [Huck](https://huckmag.com/article/snowboarder-lands-worlds-first-quad-cork-1800); [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/kleveland-lands-first-ever-quad-cork-in-a-competition)
- Cab/switch quadruple underflip 1620 (Max Parrot, Whistler April 2015; first in competition X Games 2017): 4 flips in 4.5 rotations. — [Boardworld](https://boardworld.com.au/blogs/news/max-parrot-worlds-first-switch-quadruple-underflip-1620); [Canada Snowboard](https://canadasnowboard.ca/en/news/?id=1162) (snippets)
- Cab triple underflip 1260 (Gasser), 3 inversions. — note 12-trick-fase-3 (Red Bull)
- Double McTwist 1260: 2 flips, 3.5 rotations. — [Olympics.com](https://olympics.com/en/news/shaun-white-signature-move-double-mctwist-cork)
- 2026 Olympic men's big air was won with BS 1980 melon and switch BS 1980 (Kimura). News reports do not give a cork/inversion count for these. — [FIS news](https://www.fis-ski.com/snowboard-park-and-pipe/news/2025-26/Kimura-clinches-final-run-gold-for-Japanese-1-2-in-Big-Air-thriller); [Snowboard Mag recap](https://snowboardmag.com/stories/hot-mics-a-broken-arm-and-1980s-mens-olympic-big-air-recap) (snippets)
- Searches for "double cork 720" and "double cork 900" returned no snowboard trick usage, only product pages. — search, this session (absence of evidence, not proof)

### Inferences
- [D] Table of known pairs, with r = D/360:
  - cork 360: r 1, k 1 (k = floor(r))
  - cork 540: r 1.5, k 1
  - cork 720: r 2, k 1
  - DC 1080: r 3, k 2
  - TC 1440: r 4, k 3
  - QC 1800: r 5, k 4
  - QC 1980: r 5.5, k 4
  - quint 2160: r 6, k 5
  - DMcTwist 1260: r 3.5, k 2
  - triple underflip 1260: r 3.5, k 3
  - quad underflip 1620: r 4.5, k 4
- [D] Hard bound: k ≤ floor(D/360). Every flip takes at least 360° of the total count, and each one also contributes to the rotation the judges see.
- [D] Typical corks: k = ceil(r) − 1 for r ≥ 2, i.e. the rider keeps about one revolution of flat spin. With half revolutions: DC 1260 (3.5 rev, k 2), TC 1620 (4.5, k 3), QC 1980 (5.5, k 4), so k = floor(r − 0.5) works for corks ≥ 1080. Single corks 360/540/720 all have k = 1.
- [I] "Double cork 720" (2 inversions in 2 revolutions) is geometrically at the bound. It would be a double flip with almost no spin, which riders call a "double backflip" or "double wildcat" rather than a cork. No contest use found. Treat it as **not a real trick name**.
- [I] "Double cork 900" (2 inversions in 2.5 revolutions) is allowed by the bound but not found in snowboard sources. Treat it as **rare or non-standard**: a game can allow it but should not expect riders to aim for it. The smallest standard double is the DC 1080.
- [I] Likewise "triple cork 1260" (3 in 3.5) exists only in the **underflip** family (Gasser's cab triple underflip 1260; the women's "cab triple 1260" in note 12 is the same trick). The smallest standard triple cork is the 1440. "Quad cork 1620" exists only as Parrot's quad underflip.
- [I] "Cork 360 with 1 inversion": a "cork 360" guide exists (Ora, cited in note 12), but at 1 revolution the rider is only slightly corked. Whether the head truly goes under is source-dependent (WSF: "tilted but not fully inverted"). For the game, credit k = 1 only if the up-vector actually drops below the horizon, otherwise it is a "corked 360" with k = 0.
- [I] The 2340's inversion count is unknown. By the cork pattern (k = floor(r − 0.5)) it would be a quint (k = 5) at 6.5 revolutions. That is a guess, not a fact.

### Gaps
- No source with inversion counts for 1980 Olympic runs, the 2340 or the FS 2160.
- No explicit statement from FIS/WSF of the k ≤ floor(D/360) bound. It is my derivation.

## Q3. Family definitions beyond corks (misty, rodeo, underflip, McTwist, Haakon, Michalchuk, flips)

### Cited Findings
- **Misty**: looks like a rodeo mid-trick, but "the rider uses more of a frontflip motion upon initiating the trick". FS misty is off the toes only (FIS). — [FIS list](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html) (snippet). Wikipedia calls it "an off-axis backside 540° rotation", first done by Jason King (Sugarbush halfpipe, ~1991). — [Wikipedia Misty flip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misty_flip) (snippet). CONFLICT with WSF ("partially inverted Frontside 540", see note 12).
- Misty 720 = "frontflip + 720°" (ski source). Switch double misty 1260/1440 = "spinning 1260° with two frontflips" (freeski listing). No snowboard-specific double misty source was found. — [Snowplanet/localmatters](https://www.localmatters.co.nz/sport/misty-landed-at-snowplanet); [Mountainwatch](https://www.mountainwatch.com/Snow-news/switch-450-onto-the-disaster-box-freeski-tricks/) (snippets)
- **Underflip**: "basically an inverted frontside 540 off the heel edge". Cab underflip = switch version. Prerequisites are switch 540 and flat FS 5s plus backflips/wildcats. — [Whitelines How to Underflip 540](https://whitelines.com/trick-tips/kicker-trick-tips/school-spin-frontside-rowan-coultas.html/2); [Whitelines slo-mo cab underflip](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/slo-mo-trick-tips-cab-underflip.html) (snippets). NBC: "a frontside rotation that includes a backflip in the middle of the rotation" (note 12).
- **McTwist**: "forward-flipping backside 540 done in … halfpipes and quarterpipes, rotations can exceed 540". — search summary of glossaries ([NBC](https://nbcolympics.com/post/220836)). Earlier conflict (frontflip vs backflip) is in note 12.
- **Crippler**: "an inverted 540 degree spin … performed on the frontside wall of a halfpipe". — search summary ([NBC glossary](https://nbcolympics.com/post/220836); [snowboardingprofiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions))
- **Haakon flip** (Terje Haakonsen): halfpipe, take off backwards (switch) and do "an inverted 720° rotation", landing forward. Its rotation "mimics a half-cab leading to McTwist". CONFLICT on the wall: NBC says the frontside wall, riding switch; Canada Snowboard says only "riding backwards". — [NBC glossary](https://nbcolympics.com/post/220836); [Canada Snowboard](https://canadasnowboard.ca/en/about/snowboarding/) (snippets)
- **Michalchuk** (Mike Michalchuk, late 1990s): "wildcat-style back flip off the heelside wall of a half-pipe or quarter-pipe" (Wikipedia). NBC says "backflip done in halfpipe on the backside wall", which conflicts. Variants: double Michalchuk 1080 (Taylor Gold) and a triple chuck flip on a hip (Sven Thorgren). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Michalchuk); [NBC](https://www.nbcsportsbayarea.com/news/sports/beijing-winter-olympics/triple-cork-1080-chicken-salad-snowboarding-terms-explained/); [TGR Thorgren](https://www.tetongravity.com/snowboarder-sven-thorgren-lands-the-first-triple-chuck-flip-on-a-hip) (snippets)
- **Lando roll**: listed as a FS cork 540/720 single-cork variant. — [snowboardingprofiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/?p=1531985) (snippet, low authority)
- **Tamedog**: the frontflip version of the wildcat, a cartwheel over the nose. — search summary (snowboardingprofiles)
- "Front 9", "frontflip 720" and similar ski-style names: **no snowboard source found**. Snowboard does not use "front/back + degrees" for flip-spins. Spinning frontflips are named misty (off the toes, FS) or McTwist (pipe, BS + frontflip). Spinning backflips are named rodeo, underflip, Haakon or Michalchuk-with-spin. — absence in searches this session

### Inferences
- [I] The family is defined by takeoff, i.e. by the direction of the flip component relative to the spin:
  - **cork**: shoulder drop with a spin edge. FS heel, BS toe. Axis between vertical and horizontal.
  - **rodeo**: backflip-led. FS rodeo with a FS spin, BS rodeo with a BS spin. Axis close to horizontal, the flip clearly visible.
  - **misty**: frontflip-led, FS spin, toe edge.
  - **underflip**: FS spin with a backflip in the middle; the board passes overhead "under" the rider's back. Heel edge (Whitelines). Usually cab.
  - **McTwist** = pipe BS 540 with a frontflip (the halfpipe counterpart of the misty).
  - **Haakon** = pipe switch/half-cab start with a backflip, 720.
  - **Michalchuk** = pipe backflip, wildcat-style.
  - **Crippler** = pipe FS-wall inverted 540.
  - **backflip / wildcat / frontflip / tamedog**: pure flips, k = 1, D counted as 360 (they are not usually given a degree number).

### Inferences (timeline model for the game)
- [D] Cork head-under position. With a fixed tilt θ, the head is lowest about half a revolution after the axis leans over. In a real single cork the dip happens in the **middle** of the trick: around 180–360° in a cork 540, the first ~1–1.5 revolutions in a cork 720. In multiple corks the dips come once per revolution, and the **last revolution** (or half-revolution) is flat to level out. Coaching cue: "finish the first cork by 630°, even 540°" (Newschoolers, note 12).
- [D] Spotting. The landing window opens about 90° before the end, at D − 90°. FS spins arrive "blind" in the last 90° when D is a whole multiple of 360 (360/720/1080…). BS spins arrive blind when D ends in 180 (BS 180/540/900…) (12-trick-fase-3). Misty and McTwist are frontflip-led, so the landing is seen upside down halfway through.
- [I] "Flat" vs "corked" for the small tricks. Rodeo 540 (FIS: backflip + FS 180, "opposed to the FS cork 5 which is off axis") is the *most flipped*: the axis is near horizontal and the board goes clearly over the head. Misty 540 is similar but forward-led. Underflip 540 is a FS spin with a backflip in the middle. Cork 540/720 is the *least flipped*: axis tilted, "feet above head" but not fully upside down (WSF). Proposed tilt from vertical, [D] from the cone geometry in note 12, no measured values: cork 360 ≈ 30–45°, cork 540/720 ≈ 45–60°, multi-corks ≈ 55–70°, rodeo/misty/underflip ≈ 70–85°, flips 90°.
- [I] Grab. FIS judging favours long, tweaked grabs. Melon, mute/Weddle, indy and Japan are the grabs actually named with big-air spins (Kimura BS 1980 melon, Ogiwara 2340 mute/melon).
