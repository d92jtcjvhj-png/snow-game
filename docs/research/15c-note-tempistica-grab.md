## Q1. Coaching cues: early vs late grab, "set the spin, then grab", reaching down vs bringing the board up

### Takeaway
Coaches agree on the order **pop, then start the rotation, then grab**. The grab comes once the knees are up, and the board is brought up to the hand instead of the hand reaching down. Grabbing too early (before or during the pop) weakens the pop and the spin. Reaching down bends the rider at the waist, pushes the weight forward and throws off the axis, so the spin feels "killed". A "late" grab (taken after much of the rotation, e.g. a late method on a sw BS 540) is a recognised style variant, not the default.

### Cited Findings
- "POP first, then ROTATE." Reach for the grab only once the knees have lifted and the rotation has started — [Snowboard Addiction, How to 360 with grabs (tramp)](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-360-with-grabs) (snippet)
- New riders doing 360s with grabs "would often reach down and grab their board TOO EARLY in the rotation". After starting the spin, keep the head and upper body upright and pull the board up to the grabbing hand — [Snowboard Addiction, How to 360 with grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/tramp-board-training/how-to-360-with-grabs) (snippet)
- "Lift your knees and grab your board only when your knees are up to your chest" — [Snowboard Addiction, How to stall out your 360s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/75923652-how-to-stall-out-your-360s) (snippet)
- Bending down moves the weight forward and costs balance. Pull the legs up to the chest and bring the board up to the hand. This is the topic of the coaching video "Stop Reaching Down For Your Grabs (Do This Instead)" — [Snowboard Addiction](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/stop-reaching-down-for-your-grabs-do-this-instead); [Snowboard Addiction, Basic grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/basic-grabs-on-a-snowboard) (snippets)
- Grabbing early takes away from your pop. Get height first, grab second — [search summary of coaching pages, e.g. Ridestore Mag](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-do-snowboard-grabs) (snippet; exact attribution unclear)
- Wait until the pop and spin are set before reaching. With a balanced rotation almost any grab is possible. Another commenter tells a rider who was "half popping and setting midway" to "REALLY think about pop THEN set" — [Newschoolers, Spin help](https://newschoolers.com/forum/thread/771246/Spin-help) (forum, anecdotal, ski)
- Looking down throws you off axis. One source suggests reaching for the grab as the board nears 180°, with the head turned into the spin — [Whitelines, Grabs beginner's guide](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/basics/snowboard-grabs-a-beginners-guide.html/3); [Newschoolers, Going off axis when spinning](https://www.newschoolers.com/forum/thread/590698/Going-off-axis-when-spinning) (snippets; attribution between the two is uncertain)
- Reaching straight down and missing means you "have to fish for it, and you usually don't have that much time" — [Newschoolers, Going off axis when spinning](https://www.newschoolers.com/forum/thread/590698/Going-off-axis-when-spinning) (forum, ski)
- Reaching for a grab mid-spin can twist the upper body back, so the hips stop following and the spin dies ("by doing a grab, you lose speed and you can't complete the spin") — [Snowboard Dojo Wiz](http://www.snowboarddojowiz.com/blog/how-to-do-a-controlled-spin-with-a-grab) (already cited in doc 12)
- Late-grab style: Red Bull names Torgeir Bergrem's switch backside 540 to **late method** as one of the most stylish tricks, an example of style beating raw difficulty — [Red Bull, snowboard tricks to learn](https://www.redbull.com/ie-en/snowboard-tricks-to-learn) (snippet)
- On a 180 safety grab (ski), start the spin right away and set the grab toward the side you face as you turn; "the grab sets the 180" — [Ski Mag, How to 180 safety grab](https://www.skimag.com/uncategorized/how-to-180-safety-grab/?scope=anon) (snippet)
- Stalled 360: the upper body turns into the rotation faster while the lower body stays back in the stalled position (counter-rotation), then "at the very last moment, the lower body comes around" — [Snowboard Addiction, Stall out your 360s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/75923652-how-to-stall-out-your-360s) (snippet)
- Method = melon plus backbend plus shifty (board turned about 90° and brought back for landing). A first method often shows a small shifty, and more counter-rotation makes it bigger — [Wikipedia, Method grab](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Method_grab); [Snowboard Addiction, How to method](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-method) (snippets)

### Inferences
- "Early grab" in coaching has two meanings:
  1. **Bad early**: reaching before or during the pop. This costs pop height and spin.
  2. **Good early**: grabbing right after the rotation is set (knees up), then holding long. Judges want this.
- **The "grab kills the spin" puzzle.** Physics says a tuck speeds up the spin, so why do riders feel the opposite? Two mechanisms (DERIVATION from the cited cues):
  - (a) Reaching down is a **pike at the waist**. The trunk and head move sideways, away from the spin axis (roughly the long axis of the body for a flat spin), so the yaw inertia I_yaw goes up, not down.
  - (b) The upper body **counter-rotates** to reach the board, which uses up the twist that was carrying the hips. A rider whose spin is driven only by the shoulders (weak L, partly "wind-up" style) gets stalled.
  - Bringing the board up keeps the trunk upright and pulls the legs toward the axis, so I goes down and ω goes up.
- "Stalling" and "tweaking to slow" are about **visual pace**. A shifty or stall is counter-rotation between upper and lower body; total L does not change. A tweak that extends a leg (bone or poke) moves mass away from the axis and slows ω.

## Q2. Typical timing in big air 1080–1800: when pros grab and release, how long grabs are held

### Takeoff
No measured grab-on and grab-off times (s, % of air time, degrees) for elite big air were found in any source. Every number below is an **ESTIMATE** or **DERIVATION**, built from cited air times plus coaching cues ("grab once the rotation is set", "release once you spot the landing").

### Cited Findings
- About 2.4 s of air time on an X Games big air jump — [Wired (mirror), Physics of a triple cork 1620](https://wirednews.pages.dev/2015/01/x-games-snowboarding-big-air) (snippet, 2015)
- A 1980–2160 spin was described as about 6.5 rotations over about 2.5 s after a fall from about 65 ft — [Defector, How big can big air go](https://defector.com/how-big-can-big-air-go) (snippet; context possibly freeski)
- 3.1 s hang time on the 2006 Red Bull Gap Session jump; a physicist suggests about 3 s is near the practical limit — [Snowboarder](https://www.snowboarder.com/snowboarders/we-got-a-physicist-to-calculate-how-many-corks-are-possible-on-a-snowboard) (via doc 12)
- Jiang et al. 2025 (Applied Sciences 15(12):6618): 11 Chinese national-team athletes did BS 720/1080/1440/1800 onto airbags on a dry slope, filmed with 3D motion capture **at takeoff only**. Moment of inertia, propulsion and rotational energy increased with difficulty — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/12/6618); [DOAJ record](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-25bc0faeada0432fb6681bbe172355a5) (abstract snippets). This study covers **takeoff, not the grab phase**.
- Grab first, then spot and release: "Once you spot the landing, release the grab and open your arms to check the rotation" — [Snowboard Addiction, How to 360 with grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-360-with-grabs) (snippet)
- Corks: riders aim to finish each cork early and spot the landing about 270° before touchdown — [doc 12 off-axis notes](../../docs/research/12-note-off_axis_tricks.md) (secondary; cites coaching snippets)

### Inferences (ESTIMATE / DERIVATION)
- **ESTIMATE, timeline for a big air trick with t_air ≈ 2.5–3.0 s**:

  | Phase | Time after takeoff | % of air time | Rotation done |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Pop, rotation set, knees coming up | 0–0.20 s | 0–8 % | 0–90° (shoulders lead) |
  | Grab on (hand meets board) | 0.20–0.40 s | 8–15 % | 90–180° (1080), 90–270° (1800) |
  | Grab held | about 1.6–2.0 s | about 60–75 % | most of the rotation |
  | Release (after spotting) | 2.0–2.5 s (0.35–0.6 s before touchdown) | 75–88 % | last 90–270° still to go |
  | Open, extend, absorb | last 0.3–0.5 s | 12–20 % | final 45–180° at lower ω |

  This agrees with the doc 14c estimate (release at about 70–85 % of air time, 60–120° before the final orientation on smaller spins).
- **DERIVATION**: if ω_grab is about 1.5 × ω_open (inertia ratio about 1 : 1.5, doc 14c), then the share of rotation done while grabbed is larger than the share of time grabbed. Holding 65 % of the time at 1.5× speed covers about 0.65·1.5 / (0.65·1.5 + 0.35·1) ≈ **74 %** of the degrees.
- **ESTIMATE**: on a park kicker (t_air ≈ 1.0–1.5 s), a 360/540 grab is held about 0.4–0.8 s (40–60 % of air time). Riders grab at about 90° and release at about 270° on a 360.

### Gaps
- No frame-by-frame video analysis of elite grab-on and grab-off timing was found. A measurement from Olympic footage at 25–60 fps would answer this directly. It would be a project task, not something found online.
- Full Jiang 2025 tables (flight time per trick, angular momentum) were not retrieved (mdpi blocked).

## Q3. Judging: are long, early grabs rewarded?

### Takeaway
Yes, qualitatively. FIS and broadcaster guides say grabs should be held "properly", "for long enough" or "as long as possible". Missing a grab or letting go early is penalised, and tweaks earn extra credit. No rule gives a numeric threshold (seconds or %). A 2026 Frontiers interview and survey study of big air judges exists, but its grab-specific results could not be read.

### Cited Findings
- Execution includes whether grabs were held properly and for a long enough time. "Missing the grab or letting go of the grab early will be penalized" (halfpipe wording, NBC) — [NBC Olympics, Snowboarding 101 rules](https://www.nbcolympics.com/news/snowboarding-101-rules) (snippet)
- "Grabs should be held for as long as possible, and 'tweaking' or 'poking' your trick will earn you extra points." Tail, nose and crossed-arm grabs count as harder — [Snow Magazine, Big Air event guide](https://www.snowmagazine.com/news/big-air-event-guide-winter-olympics) (snippet)
- FIS Judges Handbook 2022: grabs can change a trick's difficulty, and "it is difficult to maintain a held grab". The criteria are Execution, Difficulty, Amplitude, Variety and Progression — [FIS SB/FK Judges Handbook 2022](https://assets.fis-ski.com/f/252177/7c81eac52f/fis_sb_fk-judgeshandbook_update_spring-2022.pdf) (via doc 12)
- New, rare or creative grabs can count as progression — [NBC Olympics scoring](https://www.nbcolympics.com/news/snowboarding-101-scoring) (snippet)
- 2026 study: "Investigations of the scoring perspectives for snowboard big air based on interview and evaluation survey", Frontiers in Sports and Active Living 8:1793460 — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1793460/full); [SPONET record](https://sponet.de/sponet/Record/4097624). An earlier note in this repo (doc 14c) records, from snippets, that judges cite **long grab duration, good grab position and a firmly held board** as positive — [PMC mirror](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13111472/) (not re-verified here)
- X Games 2026: judges rewarded a hard grab even when the landing was flawed. Hunter Henderson and Mac Forehand both missed the grab on switch triples. At the Olympic qualifier, commentary said grabs mattered more than an extra 180 — [Downdays](https://www.downdays.eu/?p=179223); [Freeskier](https://www.freeskier.com/men-s-olympic-big-air-qualifiers-turn-into-a-battle-royale-for-finals-spots) (snippets, freeski)
- Mac Forehand: "If I miss my grab, I can't do it" — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/sports/olympics/mac-forehand-gold-medal-winter-olympics-milan-cortina-ski-rcna219232) (snippet, freeski)

### Inferences
- For game scoring, a grab-quality term proportional to **held time / air time** (with a bonus for an early grab-on and a penalty for a release before about 60 % of air time) fits the judging language. The weights would be game design (ESTIMATE).

### Gaps
- No numeric grab-duration criterion exists in any accessible FIS text.
- Frontiers 2026 results tables were not read.

## Q4. Does grabbing change the axis? Toe vs heel, one-handed asymmetry

### Takeaway
Gymnastics and diving biomechanics (Yeadon) give a clear rule: an **asymmetric arm or hip movement in flight tilts the body**, and while rotating that tilt shows up as twist or off-axis motion, with L conserved. So a one-handed grab on one side (or a pike toward the toe or heel edge) should, in principle, tilt the spin axis relative to the body. Snowboard sources only describe this as "looking down or reaching throws you off axis". There is no measurement for snowboarding.

### Cited Findings
- Raising one arm laterally and lowering the other in a plain jump tilts the whole body to keep zero angular momentum. During a somersault the same movement makes a tilt, and the body twists to keep L constant. More tilt means faster twist, and asymmetric movements can also remove the tilt and stop the twist — [Yeadon, Loughborough: Physics of twisting](https://www.lboro.ac.uk/microsites/ssehs/biomechanics/papers/twistphysics00.pdf); [Yeadon, twisting in flight](https://www.lboro.ac.uk/microsites/ssehs/biomechanics/papers/twistflight97.pdf); [Yeadon & Hiley, limits of aerial twist techniques](https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/The_limits_of_aerial_techniques_for_producing_twist_in_forward_1_somersault_dives/9611402/files/17258561.pdf) (snippets; simulation and theory, not snowboard data)
- Reaching down or looking down throws the rider off axis — [Whitelines beginner's guide](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/basics/snowboard-grabs-a-beginners-guide.html/3); [Snowboarding Forum, Grabs and flexibility?](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/grabs-and-flexibility.22416/) (via doc 14c, anecdotal)
- Grabbing can help stop a rider going off axis — [Ridestore Mag](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-do-snowboard-grabs/) (via doc 12)

### Inferences
- **DERIVATION**:
  - **Toe-edge grabs** (indy, mute) are taken in front of the body. They bring the knees and board up in front, a slight forward pike, so the body's long axis tips **forward/back** relative to L.
  - **Heel-edge grabs** (melon, stalefish, method) pull the board behind, with an arched back for the method. The tip goes the opposite way.
  - **One-handed grabs** (all common ones) are lateral asymmetries. The arm crosses one side and the free arm goes the other, which gives a small side tilt.
  - In flat spins the effect is probably a few degrees (**ESTIMATE 2–8°** of axis wobble). It is not a technique for steering corks.
  - For the game, this can be animation-only, or a tiny body-frame tilt with L kept exactly constant. Project rule 5 (L fixed at pop) is consistent with this.
- The coaching cue "keep the upper body upright, bring the board up" is the practical way to keep the grab symmetric enough not to tilt the axis.

### Gaps
- No snowboard IMU or video study quantifying axis tilt from grab side was found.

## Q5. Is rotation visibly faster during the grab? Angular velocity measurements

### Takeaway
Physics (L = Iω, L conserved) and coaching both say a tuck or grab speeds the spin and opening slows it. One ski forum says the opposite ("the grab will slow your rotation, so set the spin harder"). This fits the reaching-down mechanism in Q1. **No published IMU or video data showing ω rising at grab-on and falling at release** was found. IMU methods exist for snowboard spins and could measure it.

### Cited Findings
- L is set at takeoff. Arms or legs in means faster, out means slower — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/olympic-big-air-snowboarders-use-physics-to-their-advantage/); [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/61854-physics-olympics-winter-tricks-spin.html) (via doc 12)
- On big spins, pulling the nose toward the body speeds up the middle of the spin, then letting go and opening up slows it into the landing — [Snowboard Dojo Wiz](http://www.snowboarddojowiz.com/blog/how-to-do-a-controlled-spin-with-a-grab) (via doc 12)
- Grab plus tweak while spinning changes the moment of inertia and rotation speed — [CASI Reference Guide](https://www.instructor-academy.com/uploads/downloads/CASI_RefGuide.pdf) (via doc 14c)
- Conflicting (ski forum): "Remember that the grab will slow down your rotation, so set the spin harder" — [Newschoolers, ski trick definitions](https://newschoolers.com/forum/thread/771292/Ski-trick-def) (snippet, anecdotal)
- Body-mounted rate gyros integrated over time classify half-pipe spins (180/360/540/720) — [Harding et al., Griffith](https://research-repository.griffith.edu.au/items/7dbe0697-cb9f-58fb-9272-5c9a8213f63d) (snippet)
- A study comparing IMUs with markerless video for snowboard freestyle rotation found angular velocity was the key indicator for increasing rotation, while air time mattered less — [ResearchGate 390411706](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390411706_Comparative_analysis_of_inertial_measurement_units_and_markerless_video_motion_capture_systems_for_assessing_rotational_parameters_in_snowboard_freestyle) (via doc 12)
- Jiang 2025: moment of inertia **at takeoff** rises with trick difficulty — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/12/6618) (snippet). Inference: riders take off "open" (high I, so a large L = Iω for the same ω) and then pull in to the grab, which multiplies ω.

### Inferences
- **DERIVATION**: with I_open : I_grab ≈ 1.5 : 1, ω jumps about 1.5× at grab-on and drops by the same factor at release. Example: a 1080 at about 400°/s open becomes about 600°/s grabbed (numbers consistent with doc 12's averages of about 500–900°/s).
- The Jiang finding (I larger at takeoff for harder tricks) supports the "**open at pop, grab to accelerate**" pattern. That is exactly ω = L/I(pose) in `src/physics/spin.ts`.
- The ski forum's "grab slows rotation" is best read as the effect of a bad grab (reaching down = pike = higher I_yaw, or counter-rotation). It is not evidence that a proper tuck grab slows the spin.

### Gaps
- No time series of ω through a grab (IMU or video) exists in the sources I could reach. Watching pro footage frame by frame would be the cheapest way to check.

## Q6. Effects on landing: holding too long, letting go too early

### Takeaway
Holding the grab too long keeps ω high into touchdown, so the rider **over-rotates** and loses the chance to extend the legs and absorb. Letting go too early drops ω for the rest of the flight, so the rider **under-rotates**, loses judging credit for grab duration, and may have to fling the arms. The coaching fix: release once you **spot the landing**, then open the arms to check the rotation.

### Cited Findings
- Over-rotation diagnosis: "Too much wind or you held the grab too long and kept spinning. Once you spot the landing, release the grab and open your arms to check the rotation" — [Snowboard Addiction, How to 360 with grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-360-with-grabs) (snippet)
- Over-rotation is often caused by too much force in the initial spin. Keeping the head turning with the rotation over-rotates the trick, and locking the eyes on the landing helps stop it — [Ridestore, How to rotate on a snowboard](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-rotate-on-a-snowboard/); [Coachweb, Gian Simmen trick tip](https://coachweb.com/exercises/sport-workouts/1752/gian-simmen-snowboard-trick-tip) (snippets; attribution between them uncertain)
- If you don't reach for a grab, the rotation goes by too slowly — [Newschoolers, Cork 720 help](https://www.newschoolers.com/threads/cork-720-help.812913/) (via doc 14c, ski, anecdotal)
- "Spot your landing, release the grab, and orient your body for touch down. Keep your knees loose to absorb impact" — [Snowboarder Mag, Method air](https://www.snowboarder.com/how-to/master-method-air-like-a-pro-step-by-step-guide) (via doc 14c)
- Releasing early is a judging penalty — [NBC Olympics rules](https://www.nbcolympics.com/news/snowboarding-101-rules) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **DERIVATION for the game**: release time t_r sets the remaining rotation, Δθ_rest = ω_open·(t_land − t_r). The player trades judging credit (hold longer) against landing accuracy (release with the right Δθ_rest). This matches the landing bands in `src/physics/landing.ts` (signed residual yaw).
- **ESTIMATE**: releasing about 0.1 s too late on a big-air spin at about 600°/s grabbed versus about 400°/s open adds about 20° of over-rotation. Releasing about 0.3 s too early costs about 60°.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies landing-error degrees against release timing.

## Q7. Supporting evidence: freestyle skiing and skateboarding vert

### Takeaway
Freeski coaching mirrors snowboarding: set the spin, then grab. The grab is central to judging, and riders say they need it to complete the trick (Forehand). In skateboarding the classic frontside air was historically grabbed **before** take-off. Grab timing is treated as a style variable. In a frontside 540 the grab can even **change hands** mid-rotation (surf/skate analogy). Nothing quantitative was found.

### Cited Findings
- Set the full rotation first, then spot the tail until you have the grab (ski cork 720 blunt) — [Newschoolers video](https://www.newschoolers.com/videos/watch/983491/HOW-TO-CORK-720-BLUNT-GRAB-on-Skis-) (via doc 14c)
- Wait until pop and spin are set before grabbing — [Newschoolers, Spin help](https://newschoolers.com/forum/thread/771246/Spin-help) (forum)
- In the early years of the frontside air, all skaters grabbed the board before lifting off — [Wikipedia, Aerial (skateboarding)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_(skateboarding)) (snippet)
- Changing grab timing "can dramatically affect the look and style of the trick". Practise the grab on 180s and 360s before 540s — [Red Bull, The art of the ollie grab](https://redbull.com/us-en/the-art-of-the-ollie-grab) (snippet; attribution approximate)
- Kelly Slater's frontside 540 (surf): a regular frontside grab out of the lip, then about two-thirds through the spin the leading hand attaches in slob position — [Stab Mag](https://stabmag.com/?p=19778) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Across board sports, the grab is a **mid-flight, post-setup** action for spins. "Grab before take-off" only exists where the grab itself carries the board off the lip (skate vert, low-speed tricks). It is not how big air spins work.

### Gaps
- No skate or ski IMU data on ω changes through grabs.
- No freeski-specific article on grab release timing was found.
