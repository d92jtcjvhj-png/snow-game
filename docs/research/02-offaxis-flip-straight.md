## Q1: Takeoff edge per off-axis trick (regular rider) and how the tilt is created

### Takeoff
Corks follow the edge rule of their flat-spin parent: frontside cork = heel-edge pop (classic setup), backside cork / BS double cork = toe-edge pop. The axis tilt comes from the upper body. The lead arm and shoulder are thrown down (BS cork, toward the rear foot), or the lead shoulder drops into a backflip-like motion (FS cork). The deeper the drop, the more off-axis. Rodeo/misty/underflip edge conventions conflict between sources (details below).

### Cited Findings
- Frontside cork 540: classic frontside setup carve, lean back at the lip to pop off the heel edge and tail, drive the back leg toward the lead shoulder. Prerequisites: solid flat FS 5s plus backflips/wildcats. — [Snowboard Addiction, How to FS cork 540](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-frontside-cork-540) (via search snippet)
- FS cork: the flip starts by dropping the lead shoulder into a backflip-style motion as the frontside spin begins. — [Snowboard Addiction](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-cork-540) (snippet)
- Backside cork 540: "Pop up from your toe edge and follow the momentum of your initiating arm with your upper body." Throw the lead arm/shoulder downward toward the rear foot. "The more that downward motion goes into the initiation, the more off axis you will be." Prerequisites: BS 540 + wildcat. Landing: hands near the toe edge to dig in and stop the rotation. — [Snowboard Addiction, How to BS cork 540](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-backside-cork-540) (snippet)
- Glossary: corkscrew = strong toe-edge takeoff into a backside spin. — [ESPN glossary](https://www.espn.com/extreme/winterx00/s/glossarysnowboarding.html) (snippet)
- Cork = off-axis spin where the feet pass above the head. — [Burton glossary](https://www.burton.com/en-us/blogs/the-burton-blog/snowboarding-terms)
- BS double cork 1080: no source found describing the takeoff edge. Katie Ormerod learned it into foam at Woodward. — [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/how-i-did-it-katie-ormerod%E2%80%99s-double-cork-1080); SA video paywalled — [SA EP14](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/latest-news/how-to-backside-double-cork-1080-learn-to-jump-ep-14)
- General backside rule: upper body starts rotation, lower body generates takeoff power. — [Red Bull BS 180](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/how-to-backside-180-snowboard)

Done off the toe edge — [Snowboarding Profiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions); [Snowboard Bible](https://snowboardbible.com/list-of-snowboarding-tricks/)

Cab underflip: flip starts just after leaving the lip as a "barrel roll" motion; pull the legs in to speed it up — [Whitelines slo-mo cab underflip](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/slo-mo-trick-tips-cab-underflip.html). CONFLICT: Glisshop glossary calls cab "a backside rotation starting in fakie" — [Glisshop](https://www.glisshop.co.uk/guides/snowboarding-glossary.html) (likely erroneous; the dominant convention is switch frontside)

### Inferences
- Game rule with strong support: off-axis spins inherit the flat-spin edge (FS spin/cork: heel; BS spin/cork: toe). The off-axis tilt is an upper-body input at pop: lead shoulder down, roll angle proportional to drop depth.
- For a FS cork, the heel-edge pop plus leaning back at the lip gives a backflip-ish (backward-pitch) component. For a BS cork, the toe-edge pop plus the lead shoulder thrown down to the rear foot gives a sideways/forward roll. Relation (inference): the edge set determines spin direction, and the pitch component comes from where the weight goes at the lip (heels/tail → backward pitch; toes/nose → forward pitch, as in misty/frontflip).
- Rodeo/underflip naming is genuinely inconsistent. A pragmatic game spec: BS rodeo = heel edge (Dopesnow/forum); underflip = toe edge, backside-type setup with the board passing under; misty = toe edge, frontflip-type pitch with frontside rotation; FS rodeo = toe edge (historical) or heel edge. Mark as variant.
- Cab cork (switch FS): by symmetry with FS cork, this would be the switch heel edge. Unsourced.

## Q2: How the approach differs from flat spins

### Takeoff
Sparse sourcing. Corks use the same entry carve as their flat parent (FS: toe-edge line then heel at the lip; BS: heel line then toe at the lip). The difference is in upper-body direction (down/across rather than level), plus leaning back (FS cork) at the lip. I found no source specifying edge-angle magnitudes or wind-up amounts for corks vs flats.

### Cited Findings
- FS setup: "Start a ways back and line up a toe edge carve to the left of the jump, transitioning to heel edge as you approach the jump." — [SA Entry paths / FS 180](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning) (snippet)
- BS setup: go wide, come in on the heel edge, shift to the toe edge reaching the jump; one smooth heel-edge turn roughly in line with the side of the jump, then the toe edge carves up and off the lip. — [SA BS 180 park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/how-to-backside-180-on-park-jumps) (snippet)
- FS off the toes variant: heel-edge setup carve transitioning to a toe-edge platform on the lip; wind up, jump from the toe edge, point the back arm downhill. — [SA spin FS from toe edge](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-spin-frontside-from-the-toe-edge) (snippet)
- "Most people feel happiest throwing a frontside 540 off their heels, but if the jump requires it, get that toe edge dug in." — [Whitelines FS 540 indy](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-frontside-540-indy.html) (snippet)
- A big heelside turn at the lip and pop off the toes (FS 540 variant). — [WonderHowTo](https://snowboarding-lessons.wonderhowto.com/how-to/frontside-540-2000)
- A significant carve on the lip will spin you; spins are better started from the feet with the edge set; an ollie removes the edge you spin against. Some find a cork 7 easier than a flat 7; corked 540s come around more naturally than cork 360s. — [Forum: straight jump issues](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/straight-jump-issues.67649/); [Forum: ollie on edge](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/ollie-while-on-an-edge.279235/); [Forum: cork 360](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/ok-so-what-is-the-deal-with-a-cork-360.110769/) (anecdotal)
- Flat spins are typically used for higher rotations. — [Burton glossary](https://www.burton.com/en-us/blogs/the-burton-blog/snowboarding-terms)

### Inferences
- Implementation: reuse the flat-spin S-shaped entry (opposite-edge line, then switch to the takeoff edge on the ramp). The cork-specific parameters are a tilt of the upper-body throw vector and a pitch bias (lean back for FS, shoulder down for BS). A deeper/longer edge on the ramp = more rotational impulse.

## Q3: Straight flips (backflip, frontflip, wildcat, tamedog)

### Takeoff
Wildcat and tamedog take a straight, flat-base approach with the shoulders aligned to the board. The pop is ollie-like from the tail (wildcat) or nollie from the nose (tamedog), with arm throws setting the flip. Edge use for a regular (toe-roll) backflip is not documented in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- Wildcat: backflip with the board parallel to the riding line ("side" flip). Approach with a completely flat base, shoulders aligned over nose and tail. Lean over the tail, press up and back with the legs, throw the back arm down and lift the front arm up. Over-rotation = landing heavy on the tail. An indy grab helps. Learn it on a trampoline first. — [SA How to wildcat](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-wildcat) (snippet)
- Tamedog: cartwheel-style front flip launched from a nollie (nose). Front arm down to start the rotation; time the nollie with the arm throw. Tramp practice first. — [SA How to tamedog](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-tamedog) (snippet)
- Front flips: commit fully; use a mellow lip followed by a steeper drop-off and soft snow. — [Whitelines, Tyler Chorlton](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/front-flips-with-tyler-chorlton.html) (snippet)
- FIS: backflip = one rotation backwards over the horizontal axis (no edge specified). — [FIS P&P tricks list](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Wildcat/tamedog: straight, square, flat base; pitch from the tail/nose pop plus arm throw. Backflip (toe-roll style, board perpendicular to the rotation plane) is commonly taught off the toes/flat in coaching folklore, but this is unsourced here.

## Q4: Straight air / ollie off a kicker

### Takeoff
Consensus: point the board straight, keep the base flat (optionally light toe pressure), knees bent, torso upright, no carving on the lip. Two-foot pop vs ollie is a stylistic split.

### Cited Findings
- About 10 ft before the lip: point straight, base flat, bend the knees, torso upright. — [Ridestore how to jump](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-jump-on-a-snowboard/) (snippet)
- "Stay straight and flat and keep a little pressure on your toes, but not enough to really turn"; more balanced than going off the heels. — [REI beginner tricks](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/beginner-snowboarding-tricks.html)
- Flex into the tail and let it pop you off the jump; the back foot and tail are the spring of the ollie. — [Red Bull how to ollie](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/how-to-ollie-on-a-snowboard); [SA coast pop ollie](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-coast-pop-ollie) (snippets)
- Pop vs ollie: two-footed pop is more stable vs. an ollie for control off the lip (split). — [Forum pop vs ollie](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/pop-vs-ollie-off-jumps.23688/)

## Q5: Edge ↔ tilt direction relationship

### Takeoff
Sourced only indirectly. The edge sets the spin direction (heel → frontside, toe → backside for standard setups). Inversion and pitch come from shoulder drop and lean.

### Cited Findings
- See Q1/Q2 citations (SA FS cork heel + lean back + lead shoulder drop; SA BS cork toe + lead shoulder to rear foot; misty toes + frontflip motion; BS rodeo heel + backflip).

### Inferences
- Heel-edge pops pair with backward-pitching inverts (FS cork, BS rodeo heelside). Toe-edge pops pair with forward or under-pitching inverts (misty, BS cork with shoulder dive, underflip). Treat this as a working model, not a sourced rule.
