## Q1. When should the set-up turn start or finish? Does it depend on speed or jump size?

### Takeaway
Coaches place the timing around a landmark rather than a distance. That landmark is the "set-up point", the flat stretch between the in-run and the lip, where the set-up turn should finish. The rider then holds the takeoff edge up the transition and leaves the lip with the board pointing straight. I found no coaching source that gives a distance, a number of board lengths or a time in seconds.

### Cited Findings
- SA, backside 360: "The point of flat ground between the in run and the lip can be called the setup point". The search summary describes it as the place where the key timing happens. — [Snowboard Addiction, How To Backside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps)
- SA, entry paths: park jumps "usually have a straight approach and require proper set-up. You can do this by using a set up carve." For frontside spins, "set up with a heel turn, then a smooth toe edge carve towards the side of the jump". For backside spins, "set up with a smooth heel edge carve towards the side of the jump then transfer to your toe edge while riding up the lip." — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths For Spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- SA, backside 180: "You should only be shifting to your toe edge as you are rolling up the lip of the jump". This puts the final edge commitment on the ramp itself, not before it. — [Snowboard Addiction, Improve Your Backside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/17257876-improve-your-backside-180s)
- trick-tips.com, per the search summary: the setup point is the flat section between the run-in and the lip. The rider finishes the setup turn there, moves onto the spin edge and holds that edge up the lip. — [trick-tips.com, How to 360, 540 and 720](https://www.trick-tips.com/snowboarding/how-to-360-540-and-720-frontside-or-backside)
- PushDaLimit, per the search summary: the set-up is an S-carve that gets the rider square and on edge at the lip. The point where the edge changes moves earlier or later depending on jump size. — [PushDaLimit, How to S-Carve](https://pushdalimit.com/tutorial/how-to-s-carve-on-a-snowboard/)
- Ideal takeoff: "taking off the lip with your board perpendicular to the jump." — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- Torah Bright, backside 540 clip, per the summary: "locking in on that single line through the transition". — [Britannica video, Torah Bright](https://www.britannica.com/video/195284/Torah-Bright-Olympic-pipe-tricks-amplitude-backside)
- Snow Magazine on the transition: "a long smooth transition is generally preferable". — [Snow Magazine, What is Big Air?](https://snowmagazine.com/news/big-air-event-guide-winter-olympics)

### Inferences
- For the game, two moments matter. The set-up turn should end at the set-up point, which is roughly the base of the transition. The takeoff edge is then held, not turned, up the ramp. The turn's start therefore has to come before the base of the kicker, early enough for the arc to finish there.
- "Depends on jump size" has only one weak source (PushDaLimit). It is consistent with simple geometry: more speed means a longer arc for the same edge angle, so the start point moves further up the in-run. A model can tie the ideal window to distance, or to time-to-lip at the current speed.

### Gaps
- No source gives a numeric distance, number of board lengths or time.
- I found no AASI, BASI or CASI manual text on this.
- No source explicitly describes timing relative to the "knuckle of the takeoff".

## Q2. Effects of starting too EARLY vs too LATE

### Takeaway
The "too early" error is well documented. The board is still turning, or has already turned, on the ramp. The result is pre-rotation, a takeoff on an angle or diagonal, scrubbed speed, a knuckle landing and drifting. This matches the game rule's "too early → lateral component/drift". The "too late" error is described less directly. It shows up as a last-second speed check or skid at the lip, an unedged or skidding takeoff, and coming up short. Under-rotation is attributed mostly to the wind-up and pop, not to how long the carve was.

### Cited Findings: too early
- SA, backside 360, per the summary: "If you set up to early you will end up not going in a straight direction, and you will have made some of you rotation even before you leave the jump." — [Snowboard Addiction, Backside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps)
- SA: riders "will carve too far and end up already spinning some of the 360 and 'scrubbing' off the lip of the jump. This problem will totally kill your momentum and air stability." — [Snowboard Addiction, Frontside 360 problems](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/16098668-top-3-problems-when-learning-how-to-frontside-360-on-a-snowboard)
- SA, backside 180: "Another mistake is setting up far too early on the jump", which "will make your balance worse, and make the gap of the jump longer." The summary also says the board leaves at an angle with unwanted rotation. — [Snowboard Addiction, Improve Your Backside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/17257876-improve-your-backside-180s)
- trick-tips.com, per the summary, lists the usual causes of knuckle landings: "checking speed too close to the lip, setting up too early so the board is still turning as it leaves, or leaving the lip on an angle". A carve that is still turning at takeoff "sends you off in the wrong direction". — [trick-tips.com](https://www.trick-tips.com/snowboarding/how-to-360-540-and-720-frontside-or-backside)
- Search summary of SA content: turning or spinning too early up the face reduces speed, causes a knuckle landing, and the rider "drifts off the landing". This is a search-tool synthesis, so the exact wording is unverified. — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- Failed set-up leads to flying off on a diagonal. The board should leave perpendicular to the lip to land in the sweet spot. — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- Forum (anecdotal): a significant carve on the takeoff will generate rotation, even when a straight air is intended. — [SnowboardingForum, Straight Jump Issues](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/straight-jump-issues.67649/)

### Cited Findings: too late, rushed or skidded
- SA, frontside 360: "No last minute speed checks, just a nice smooth set up turn." — [Snowboard Addiction, Frontside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/news/how-to-frontside-360-on-park-jumps)
- Key Aspects Coaching: slipping or skidding on the lip is "one of the biggest problems I see with 360 rotations". "If we don't edge the board as we head up to jump and take off, we will slide and skid the board as we leave the lip". This can leave the rider coming up short and hitting the knuckle. — [Key Aspects Coaching, 360 Setup / Take off & Stability](https://www.keyaspectscoaching.com/freestyle-tips/tips-mondays-360-setup-take-off-stability/)
- trick-tips.com: "checking speed too close to the lip" is a cause of knuckle landings. — [trick-tips.com](https://www.trick-tips.com/snowboarding/how-to-360-540-and-720-frontside-or-backside)
- Forum (anecdotal): one rider was told to use "more of your edge to initiate the spin and then using your body in the air to keep the spin going". This suggests a weak or absent edged set-up gives less rotation help. — [SnowboardingForum, How to 360 properly?](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/how-to-360-properly.40348/)
- Forum (anecdotal): a rider who leans back and twists the front knee "right before the lip" asks whether he is starting rotation too early. This shows that a last-moment body twist is a recognised fault. — [SnowboardingForum, Problem leaning back when initiating backside spins](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/problem-leaning-back-when-initiating-backside-spins.14351/)

### Inferences
- The game rule's "too early → wide/long turn → lateral component (drift)" is supported by coaching sources. Their stated mechanism is that the board is still turning or already rotated at the lip, so the rider leaves on an angle. Further effects are speed loss (scrub), a longer gap to the landing, knuckling and drifting on landing.
- The game rule's "too late → short/cramped turn → less rotation help" is plausible but only indirectly sourced. Real-world sources describe late errors as a rushed edge change or skid on the ramp, giving a flat or skidding takeoff, less pop, coming up short and knuckling. A faithful game model could add a penalty for very late starts, such as speed loss, pop loss and a takeoff instability or skid flag, not only reduced rotation assist.
- Note the asymmetry. Coaches stress "too early" far more, probably because it is the common beginner error: over-carving or turning on the ramp.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the rotation lost from a late set-up.
- I found no source stating "late set-up → under-rotation on big spins" explicitly.

## Q3. Does a well-timed set-up add rotation on big spins? Momentum vs "don't rely on it", and wind-up timing

### Takeaway
Coaches agree the set-up carve contributes something: edge grip, a pre-loaded line and some momentum. They insist the rotation must be initiated by the upper body at the lip, with the board leaving straight. The two views fit together. The carve supplies a stable edge and some angular pre-load, while the rotation itself comes from the upper-body wind-up released at takeoff. For bigger spins, coaches say the change is in the wind-up and release, not in a bigger carve.

### Cited Findings
- SA, per the summary: a set-up carve adds momentum, but "Don't carve too hard in your set up, you don't want to rely on the set up for your rotation". "You should still be using your upper body to initiate the spin." — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- SA, frontside 180, per the summary: the toe-edge set-up carve gives edge grip but "doesn't create frontside momentum". The rider must supply it with the arms and upper body. — [Snowboard Addiction, Advanced Frontside 180](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-advanced-frontside-180-on-a-snowboard)
- SA large jumps: "right as you leave the lip of the jump, release the wind up of your arms and shoulders". For bigger spins, "the faster you release your wind up, the quicker you will spin." — [Snowboard Addiction, How To Spin 360s, 540s & 720s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps)
- trick-tips.com, per the summary: the same line works for every spin size. What changes with bigger spins is how hard the rider releases at the lip and where they spot the landing. — [trick-tips.com](https://www.trick-tips.com/snowboarding/how-to-360-540-and-720-frontside-or-backside)
- Wind-up timing, per the summary: "Keep the board in a straight line and pre-wind your shoulders ... then gently dig your toe edge into the kicker and pop hard into the spin, unleashing your arms". — search result attributed to Ridestore, [How to rotate on a snowboard](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-rotate-on-a-snowboard/). The exact page attribution in the search output was unclear.
- Forum (anecdotal): the pre-wind "has to occur early enough to get a lot of tension built but not so soon you pause your motion." — [SnowboardingForum, How to get over this backside 360 bad habit?](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/how-to-get-over-this-backside-360-bad-habit.273070/)
- Forum (anecdotal): rotation must start at takeoff, because "once you launch, the rotation you have is all you've got". Build the wind-up before the lip and unwind at takeoff. — [SnowboardingForum, How to 360 properly?](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/how-to-360-properly.40348/)
- Ed Shred, per the summary: start the spin with the upper body so the board goes straight off the lip. — [Shred School, frontside tag](https://www.edshreds.com/tag/frontside/)
- Forum (anecdotal): carving keeps speed loss low, so spins need barely more speed than a straight air. — [SnowboardingForum, Proper Procedure Approaching and Hitting a kicker](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/proper-procedure-approaching-and-hitting-a-kicker.49547/)

### Inferences
- The wind-up happens during, or at the end of, the set-up turn and the ride up the transition, and is released at the lip. This supports linking "start charging" to the set-up carve in the game. The physical event that should scale rotation is the release at the lip.
- A reasonable game model would make the set-up carve give a modest rotation assist plus takeoff quality: edge hold and no skid. Most rotation should come from the charge or wind-up amount. Over-carving should convert into pre-rotation and lateral drift, not into extra air rotation.
- The claim that a well-timed set-up gives more rotation on big spins than a late one is not directly evidenced. Sources support a weaker claim: a clean, edged, well-timed set-up lets the rider pop and release fully, while a rushed or skidded one costs pop and stability.

### Gaps
- I found no biomechanics study that measures how much angular momentum comes from the approach carve versus the upper-body wind-up in snowboard spins.
- AASI, BASI and CASI material was not reachable.

## Q4. How pros time the approach on big air

### Takeaway
I found no rider interview that states when they start their carve on big air jumps. The available pro quotes are about mindset or line commitment, not set-up timing.

### Cited Findings
- Torah Bright, per the summary: lock "in on that single line through the transition", and watch the front foot leave before initiating. — [Britannica video, Torah Bright](https://www.britannica.com/video/195284/Torah-Bright-Olympic-pipe-tricks-amplitude-backside)
- Julia Marino on big air: "one massive drop-in", "hitting it cold turkey". This is about mindset, not timing. — [GearJunkie, Julia Marino interview](https://gearjunkie.com/winter/snowboarding/julia-marino-snowboarding-q-and-a-interview)
- Big-air judges' study: "high jumps, stable posture control, firm grabs, and smooth landings" are primary score predictors. Snow Magazine stresses a clean takeoff for execution. — [PMC, Scoring perspectives for snowboard big air](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13111472/); [Snow Magazine](https://snowmagazine.com/news/big-air-event-guide-winter-olympics)

### Inferences
- Clean takeoffs are rewarded in judging. That supports a game penalty, in score or stability, for the drift or diagonal takeoff caused by a too-early set-up.

### Gaps
- I found no pro quote of the form "I start my carve at the start of the transition".
- No contest-footage analysis of where pros start their set-up was found.
- Red Bull and Whitelines trick-tip pages could not be fetched.
