# Catalog of snowboard grabs: body shape, fit with each rotation, effect on the moment of inertia

Reference: **regular stance** (left foot forward). Front hand = left, rear hand = right. Toe edge = chest side, heel edge = back side. **Goofy mirrors everything**: swap left and right. The grab name does not change, because names are defined by front/rear hand and toe/heel edge, not by left/right. **Riding switch**: "front" and "rear" follow the direction of travel. A switch rider's front hand is the one nearer the new nose, which is the board's physical tail. So a switch Indy is still rear hand on the toe edge between the feet. Spin naming follows the same rule: a cab is a switch frontside spin ([snowboardingdays list](https://www.snowboardingdays.com/?p=5558)), and switch backside is the fourth spin direction ([NBC glossary via search](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/sports/beijing-winter-olympics/triple-cork-1080-chicken-salad-snowboarding-terms-explained/)).

## 1. Grab catalog: hand, spot, arm, legs, compactness, typical rotations, difficulty and judging

### Takeaway
The core of the catalog is a 2×2 grid: front or rear hand × toe or heel edge, taken between the bindings. Mute/Weddle (front, toe), Indy (rear, toe), Melon (front, heel) and Stalefish (rear, heel) are the base grabs, plus Nose and Tail at the tips. Every other grab is one of three things: a different spot (in front of or behind the bindings), a different arm path (across the body, between the legs, behind a leg), or a leg change (boned or poked). "Bad" grabs are rear-hand grabs taken behind the rear binding (Tindy, Tailfish), or grabs on the knee or binding instead of the board, and judges penalize them under execution.

### Cited findings: definitions

**The base grabs (the 2×2 grid)**
- **Indy**: rear hand on the toe edge between the bindings. Often the first grab people learn, "arguably the simplest" — [Snowboard Addiction, basic grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/basic-grabs-on-a-snowboard); [Wikipedia, Indy grab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indy_grab) (search result); simplicity per the summary of [neversummer.com](https://neversummer.com/blogs/snowboarding/easy-snowboard-tricks-to-master)
- **Mute → Weddle**: front hand on the toe edge between the bindings. It is the mirror of the Indy and is usually the second grab learned. From the 2020/21 season FIS calls it the **Weddle**, after Chris Weddle, a deaf skateboarder who originated the grab ("he is deaf, not mute") — [FIS news, Weddle grab to replace mute](https://www.fis-ski.com/snowboard-park-and-pipe/news/2020-21/weddle-grab-to-replace-mute-in-honour-of-originator-chris-weddle) (search result)
- **Melon**: front hand on the heel edge between the bindings, reaching **behind the front leg** — [NBC Olympics glossary](https://nbcolympics.com/post/220836); [FIS tricks list via search](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html)
- **Stalefish**: rear hand on the heel edge between the bindings, the arm going **around the outside / behind the rear knee**. The board is usually tweaked behind the rider — [FIS tricks list via search](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html)
- **Nose**: front hand on the nose. The legs do the work: front knee up, rear leg extended down, and the nose comes up to the hand. Keep the head up — [Snowboard Addiction, nose & tail](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-nose-tail-grab-on-a-snowboard) (via notes 14c)
- **Tail**: rear hand on the tail. If it is hard to reach, bend the rear leg more. For style, stretch out the front leg before letting go — [Whitelines, how to tail grab](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-tail-grab.html) (via notes 14c)

**Method and Japan (tweaked versions of the base grabs)**
- **Method**: front hand on the heel edge (a Melon), then tweaked. Red Bull: a Melon with a bit of backbend plus a shifty. Others: "tweaked melon", rear leg extended, "tweak the board up behind you and arch your hips forward". Variations include power method, cross-bone and Palmer method — [Red Bull infographic](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/learning-snowboard-tricks-the-easy-way-infographic); [Snowboarding Profiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions); [Wikipedia Method grab](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Method_grab) (search results)
- **Japan**: front hand on the toe edge between the feet, with the arm **wrapped around the front leg**, and the **front knee pulled down** to the board — [FIS tricks list via search](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html); [snowboardingdays](https://www.snowboardingdays.com/?p=5558). Conflict: one source calls it "a super tweaked out Mute grabbed behind your back" ([InTheSnow](https://www.inthesnow.com/guide-snowboarding-terminology/), via notes 14c).

**Crail family (rear hand in front of the front foot)**
- **Crail**: rear hand on the toe edge **in front of the front foot**, toward the nose, with the **rear leg boned**. Some riders call any rear-hand toe-side grab in front of the front foot a crail. "Nose crail" or "real crail" means the rear hand on the nose — [Snowboarding Profiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions); [Wikipedia mirror via search](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/List_of_snowboard_tricks)
- **Nose-mute / mute nose**: front hand on the toe edge in front of the front foot, or on the nose on the toe side. No source defined it directly. The name is the usual convention (mute = front hand on the toe side, nose = position) — **gap / ESTIMATE**.

**Cross-body grabs**
- **Seatbelt**: front hand reaches **across the body** to the tail — [mataceinc, 10 essential grabs](https://mataceinc.com/blogs/news/10-essential-snowboard-grabs-every-rider-should-know) (via notes 14c); [Snowboard Addiction, how to crail & seatbelt](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-crail-seatbelt) (search result)
- **Nuclear**: rear hand reaches across the front of the body to the heel edge **in front of the front foot**. Conflict: another source puts it behind the front foot — [Snowboarding Profiles via search](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/?p=1531985)

**Grabs between the legs or behind a leg**
- **Roast Beef**: rear hand **between the legs** to the heel edge between the bindings, rear leg straight — [Wikipedia mirror via search](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/List_of_snowboard_tricks)
- **Chicken Salad**: hand between the legs to the heel edge between the bindings. **Conflict on the hand**: Red Bull says the front hand. NBC Sports and the REI glossary say the rear hand with the front leg straight. The Wikipedia mirror says the name is "Neil Blender" respelled backwards — [Red Bull infographic](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/learning-snowboard-tricks-the-easy-way-infographic); [NBC Sports Philadelphia](https://www.nbcsportsphiladelphia.com/beijing-2022-winter-olympics/triple-cork-1080-chicken-salad-snowboarding-terms-explained/198491/); [REI glossary](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/snowboarding-glossary.html). Note: the FIS/Wikipedia convention (front hand between the legs to the heel edge, front leg boned) looks more common, but it was not verified on the full page.
- **Canadian Bacon**: rear hand reaches **behind the rear leg** to the toe edge between the bindings, rear leg boned — [Wikipedia mirror via search](https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/List_of_snowboard_tricks)
- **Taipan**: front hand between the legs to the toe edge between the bindings. FIS says the name comes from "tail" + "Japan air" — [FIS tricks list via search](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html)

**Heel side near the front binding**
- **Lien**: front hand on the heel edge between the binding and the nose, board flat. **Conflicts**: Snowboarding Profiles puts it in front of or behind the front binding. FIS adds rear leg bent and front leg straight. It is a skateboarding grab (Neil Blender, "lien" = "Neil" backwards) — [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/learning-snowboard-tricks-the-easy-way-infographic); [Snowboarding Profiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions); [FIS via search](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html)

**Boned, stiffy and contested grabs**
- **Slob**: **conflict**. Red Bull: a Mute/Weddle with the rear leg boned. FIS: "Slob Air", front hand at the Weddle spot, rear leg boned, board parallel to the ground — [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/learning-snowboard-tricks-the-easy-way-infographic); [FIS via search](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html)
- **Stiffy**: both legs straight while grabbing, usually a double grab ("Double Stiffy"). FIS also lists a "Weddle Stiffy" (Weddle with both legs straight) — [FIS / Snowboarding Profiles via search](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html)
- **Bloody Dracula**: **conflict**. Red Bull: nose down, both hands on the tail (rear hand tail grab, front hand reaching behind the back). The Wikipedia list gives a different definition (toe edge in front of the front foot, rear leg boned) — [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/learning-snowboard-tricks-the-easy-way-infographic); [NBC glossary](https://www.nbcolympics.com/news/snowboarding-101-glossary)

**Double grabs**
- **Rocket**: front hand on the nose and rear hand on the tail, per mataceinc (via notes 14c). Red Bull describes a two-hand nose grab and also uses "slob" for it — [mataceinc](https://mataceinc.com/blogs/news/10-essential-snowboard-grabs-every-rider-should-know); [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/learning-snowboard-tricks-the-easy-way-infographic). Conflict on the exact form.
- **Truck Driver**: double grab, one hand in front of the front binding and the other behind the rear one. FIS: "both hands grab Indy and Melon" (Melon + Indy at once). **Drunk Driver**: Stalefish + Mute at the same time — [FIS / Snowboarding Profiles via search](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html)
- **Other double grabs**: Double Indy (Human Kinetics); Beef Carpaccio = Roast Beef + Chicken Salad between the legs with the arms crossed; Beef Curtains = Grosman + Roast Beef — [Human Kinetics](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/performing-grabs-including-the-double-indy-and-method-grabs); [Snowboarding Profiles via search](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/?p=1531985)

**Bad grabs**
- **Tindy**: rear hand on the toe edge **between the rear binding and the tail**, "the ugly brother of the Indy… poor form". **Tailfish**: rear hand on the heel edge behind the rear binding, a "bad stalefish" — [Snowboarding Profiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions); [Snowboard Addiction, complicated world of grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/112716804-the-complicated-world-of-snowboard-grabs) (via notes 14c)

**No grab: shifty**
- **Shifty**: no grab. The upper body counter-rotates against the lower body in the air, then returns. Part of the Method according to Red Bull — [Red Bull infographic](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/learning-snowboard-tricks-the-easy-way-infographic). The definition of the shifty itself was not verified on the full page.

**Tweaks, bones and pokes**
- Tweak = stretching the body to exaggerate a grab. Bone = kicking a leg straight once the grab is locked in — [Snowboard Addiction, how to tweak & bone](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/grabs-1/how-to-tweak-bone-your-tricks)
- **Indy tweak**: straighten the rear leg, tuck the front leg, reach toward the bindings for the full stretch — [Ridestore, how to do grabs](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-do-snowboard-grabs)
- **Mute tweak / boned mute**: rear leg extended — [Snowboard Addiction](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/112716804-the-complicated-world-of-snowboard-grabs)
- **Tail poke**: rear leg straight, tail pushed away. **Nose poke**: front leg straight, nose pushed away, usually while grabbing it — [CBC glossary](https://www.cbc.ca/sports/2.722/snowboard-glossary-1.854390); [InTheSnow](https://www.inthesnow.com/guide-snowboarding-terminology/) (via notes 14c)
- **Method** and **Stalefish tweak**: board tweaked behind the rider, hips pushed forward — [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/learning-snowboard-tricks-the-easy-way-infographic); [FIS via search](https://www.fis-ski.com/DB/snowboard/park-and-pipe/tricks-list.html)

### Cited findings: judging and difficulty
- In slopestyle and big air, riders are expected to grab on every jump. **A missed grab, or letting go early, is penalized.** Judges check under execution that the grab is held properly and long enough — [NBC Olympics scoring guide](https://nbcolympics.com/post/220821); [LEX18 snowboarding 101 scoring](https://www.lex18.com/news/snowboarding-101-scoring)
- A grab raises difficulty, and **nose and tail grabs, or grabs with the arms crossed, count as harder** than the others — [LEX18 / KSBY snowboarding 101 scoring](https://www.ksby.com/news/snowboarding-101-scoring) (broadcast explainer, not an FIS document)
- FIS handbook, quoted by Snow Magazine: a trick, variation **or grab** that is "new, unique, rare, innovative or creative for that level" can be recognized as **progression** — [Snow Magazine, big air event guide](https://www.snowmagazine.com/news/big-air-event-guide-winter-olympics)
- Criteria: execution, difficulty, amplitude, variety and progression, weighed together and without fixed points. Sources differ on the acronym (DEAL, DAVE-P) — [LEX18](https://www.lex18.com/news/snowboarding-101-scoring); [Snow Magazine](https://www.snowmagazine.com/news/big-air-event-guide-winter-olympics)
- Beijing 2022 slopestyle case: Max Parrot grabbed the **knee/binding instead of the board** on a cork 1620 and was not penalized. Head judge Iztok Sumatic admitted the judges missed it in the camera angle they had. One estimate put the penalty at about 3 points, which would have meant gold for Su Yiming. **The grab must be on the board, in the right place** — [ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/olympics/story/_/id/33267181/olympic-judging-scrutiny-max-parrot-slopestyle-win); [SCMP](https://amp.scmp.com/sport/china/article/3166503/winter-olympics-snowboard-judge-admits-maradona-hand-god-error-cost-su); [CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2022-02-10/Head-snowboard-judge-explains-calls-during-slopestyle-final-17xKCwQD8OY/index.html)
- The Tindy is "poor form" (ugly brother of the Indy) — [Snowboarding Profiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE**: with a few exceptions (Japan, Truck Driver), grabs with a boned or straight leg (Method, Slob, Lien, Stiffy, Canadian Bacon, Crail, pokes) are style grabs. They are held on straight airs and slower spins. Grabs with both knees bent (Indy, Weddle, Melon, double grabs) are the ones used on high spins, where you have to tuck.
- **ESTIMATE**: "rare" grabs (between the legs, across the body) are a path to the progression and variety criteria, not to raw difficulty.

### Gaps
- No full page was read: exact definitions of Japan, Lien, Slob, Bloody Dracula, Nuclear and Chicken Salad stay in conflict.
- Nose-mute has no direct source.
- No public FIS document with a "difficulty ranking" of grabs was found. Only broadcast explainers say nose/tail/crossed arms are harder.

## 2. Which grabs pros use with which spins, and why (including elite big air 1440–2340)

### Takeaway
The coaching rule is about **shoulders**. The grab should keep the shoulders "open" in the direction of rotation. Snowboard Addiction says Indy and Melon for frontside, Weddle and Stalefish for backside. For learners, Melon and Indy are the go-to grabs in both directions. At elite level (1620–2340) the rule becomes much looser: you see Indy, Weddle, Melon, Stalefish, Tail and Nose on every direction. What they share is that the grab is **compact and stable** (between the feet, or tail/nose with tucked legs). Grab choice there is about difficulty/variety (doing the same spin with a different grab) and about style, not about mechanics.

### Cited findings
- Indy and Melon are common for **frontside**, because the shoulder position helps create and keep frontside momentum. Weddle and Stalefish turn the shoulders the other way and "go so well with" **backside** spins — [Snowboard Addiction, how to 360 with grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-360-with-grabs-on-a-snowboard)
- On frontside 360s, Melon or Indy are "the easiest grabs" because they keep the shoulders open to the direction of rotation — [Snowboard Addiction FS 360 / Whitelines via search](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-360-on-park-jumps)
- Melon is "a great go-to grab", and Melon or Indy are recommended first because they are very common in both directions. The easiest grab on a BS 180 will probably be the easiest on a BS 360 too — [Snowboard Addiction 360 with grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-360-with-grabs-on-a-snowboard); [Snowboard Addiction BS 180 with grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-180-with-grabs)
- Stalefish and Melon are "really simple to integrate into a 180 because the board naturally turns". There is a dedicated **BS 180 Melon** tutorial — [Snowboard Addiction, BS 180 Melon](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/how-to-backside-180-melon) (search summary)
- The FS 180 Melon is "perfectly suited" to frontside 180s for style — [Whitelines, FS 180 melon](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-frontside-180-melon.html)
- FS 360 tailgrab: hold the tail, look over the front shoulder, **let go just after 270°** to get ready for the landing — [Whitelines, FS 360 tailgrab](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/school-spin-frontside-rowan-coultas.html)
- Don't grab too early off the jump (it costs pop). Get height first, then grab — [Snowboard Addiction, basic grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/basic-grabs-on-a-snowboard)
- In FIS naming, the Indy on backside rotations has its own convention ("When performing a backside aerial or backside rotation, this grab is referred to as an Indy") — [snowboardingdays via search](https://www.snowboardingdays.com/?p=5558) (note: this phrase comes from the old frontside/backside-air convention; not verified on the full page)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE**: elite pairings contradict the "FS = Indy/Melon, BS = Weddle/Stalefish" rule in several places (BS Indy, BS Melon, FS Weddle, sw BS Stalefish). So at elite level the rule only describes the **ease** for learners. Pros pick the grab for **variety** (same spin, different grab in another run) and **difficulty** (Stalefish and the tips are harder).
- **ESTIMATE, why BS 180/360 with Melon/Stalefish**: both are heel-side grabs. On backside the rider turns their back to the landing, and a heel-side grab keeps the chest facing the toe edge and the head free to look over the leading shoulder. "The board naturally turns" (Snowboard Addiction) is consistent with this.
- **ESTIMATE, Japan with cab/corks/rodeos**: the Japan pulls the front knee down and the tail up, so the body curls into a ball around the hand. That fits inverted rotations (a ball shape with low flip inertia) and looks stylish on cab/cork spins. **I found no source that pairs Japan with cab double cork or rodeo**, so this stays a gap.
- **ESTIMATE, Indy on underflip / misty**: no source found. Mechanical logic: the Indy is the most centered and stable grab (rear hand in front, board between the feet), useful when the head is upside down. Not verified.
- **ESTIMATE, 1980–2340**: the grabs actually used (Weddle, Melon, Indy, Tail) are all "compact" grabs with both knees bent, or a tip with the leg tucked. No elite trick with a Method or a boned Stiffy was found above 1440. This is consistent with high spins needing low inertia.

### Gaps
- No primary source (pro interview) explaining **why** a pro picks a given grab for a given spin.
- Grabs for underflip, misty, rodeo and cab double cork: definitions found (rodeo = back flip + spin, misty = front-flip-like start, underflip = back flip with FS 180 on the vertical axis), but **no grab pairing**.
- Grabs in Murase's 2026 runs and in Su Yiming's 2026 1980s: not named in the snippets.

## 3. Which grabs make the body compact (faster) vs extended (slower): effect on moment of inertia

### Takeaway
The physics is solid: L is fixed at the pop and ω = L/I. Tucking (knees to chest + grab) lowers I and speeds up rotation. Opening or extending raises I and slows it down. Coaching uses this the other way around for control: no grab means a slow rotation, "letting go" means opening up and slowing down to spot the landing, and a tweak means stretching and "stalling". **I found no study that measures inertia grab by grab.** All the numbers below are **ESTIMATE**s. A key point from geometry: **the board barely changes the spin I (vertical axis) between grabs, but it changes the flip I (somersault axis) a lot.** So grab choice matters much more on flips and corks than on flat spins.

### Cited findings
- Angular momentum is conserved in the air. Pulling the limbs in lowers I and the spin has to speed up — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/olympic-big-air-snowboarders-use-physics-to-their-advantage/)
- Billy Morgan's quad cork 1800: "the smaller he can tuck—minimizing the moment of inertia—the faster he rotates", about 1.7 rev/s on average — [WIRED mirror](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/the-physics-of-one-of-the-craziest-big-air-snowboard-tricks-ever)
- Educational summary: tucking the knees and grabbing brings mass closer to the axis, "significantly reducing I and increasing spin speed" (secondary source) — [vectree](https://vectree.io/c/rider-biomechanics-and-rotational-physics)
- At **takeoff**, elite athletes (Chinese national team, BS 720–1800 onto an airbag) **increase** I as difficulty goes up. They are extended at the pop and only tuck afterward. In 157 corks by 15 riders, angular velocity, takeoff speed and moment of inertia were the key indicators of rotation amount — [Applied Sciences 2025, 15(12):6618](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/12/6618)
- Grabbing and tweaking while spinning changes I and controls rotation speed — [CASI reference guide](https://www.instructor-academy.com/uploads/downloads/CASI_RefGuide.pdf) (via notes 14c)
- Reach for the grab, or the rotation "goes by too slowly" (ski, forum) — [Newschoolers](https://www.newschoolers.com/threads/cork-720-help.812913/) (via notes 14c)
- Gymnastics somersault axis (adult male): straight 19.8 kg·m², piked 5.9, tucked 3.8 — [U. Toronto, Physics of somersaults](https://www.physics.utoronto.ca/~phy189h1/Physics%20of%20Somersaults%20and%20Twists.pdf) (via notes 14c)
- Spin control: count the rotations to slow down. Locking the eyes on the landing spot "blocks" the rotation. Looking back too early over-rotates — [Snowboard Addiction FS 540 / FS 360 via search](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-540)
- Release around 270° on a FS 360 to get ready for the landing — [Whitelines](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/school-spin-frontside-rowan-coultas.html)

### Inferences: model per grab (ESTIMATE)

**Geometry (ESTIMATE).** Rider 75 kg, board + bindings + boots about 7 kg, board 1.55 m, stance 0.55 m.
- **Spin axis** (vertical, through the CoM, roughly along the torso): the board is perpendicular to the axis. Its contribution, about m·L²/12 ≈ 7·1.55²/12 ≈ **1.4 kg·m²**, is **the same whatever the grab**, because the board stays across the axis. The legs stay roughly along the axis. What changes is:
  - the **arms**: a free arm held straight out is about 4 kg at r ≈ 0.35–0.45 m, so +0.5–0.8 kg·m², and grabbing pulls the arm in;
  - **torso lean / backbend** (Method), which moves mass off the axis;
  - **board pitch** (pokes, Bloody Dracula, Japan with the tail up): if the board tilts by angle θ toward the vertical, its spin contribution drops by cos²θ. **A poke therefore slightly lowers I_spin** (counterintuitive), but the boned leg and arched torso push it back up.
  - Overall effect on I_spin between grabs: about **±15–25 %**.
- **Flip axis** (somersault, mediolateral to the body ≈ along the board's length): the board lies **parallel** to the axis, so its contribution is about m·d², where d is the board's distance from the CoM. Straight body: d ≈ 0.9 m → ≈ 5.7 kg·m². Tucked with a grab between the feet: d ≈ 0.45–0.5 m → ≈ 1.5 kg·m². **Pulling the knees up decides almost everything here**, with a 2–4x factor between tucked and extended (consistent with the gymnastics tuck : pike : straight ratio of about 1 : 1.5 : 5, reduced by the board, which limits how far you can tuck).
- **Cork** (tilted axis): a mix of the two. The more inverted the trick, the more the grab matters.
