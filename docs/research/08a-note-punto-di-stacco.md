**Verdict on the designer's claim, in short.** The claim is **partly supported**:
- Supported as a description of what happens. Where the rider crosses the lip, and at what heading, depends on the approach line and on the timing and size of the set-up carve. An early or too-hard carve gives a diagonal exit and sideways drift that can carry the rider "off the side of the jump".
- Not supported as a description of the intended technique. Every coaching source treats an off-axis or angled exit as an **error**. The target is the board "straight", "perpendicular to the lip", heading for "the middle of the landing".
- So the realistic model is this. The intended takeoff is centred and square. Variation across the lip and in exit angle comes from execution error, and its size grows with how early or how hard the set-up carve is.

## Q1. Do riders take off from the side of the lip or at an angle? Is it common, an error, or a deliberate technique?

### Takeaway
On standard kickers and hips, an angled takeoff is described as **common among beginners and wrong**. The prescribed technique is to leave the lip straight and make any change of direction in the air. The only "deliberate angle" evidence is anecdotal and concerns a slight heel-edge angle to start a spin, on smaller jumps. I found no source describing pros deliberately using one side of a wide big-air kicker.

### Cited Findings
- On hips, Snowboard Addiction (SA) says beginners take off at an angle and that this is incorrect: "Many beginner park riders takeoff the Hip at an angle, but this is the incorrect way to hit a Hip jump. Hitting a Hip at an angle shoots you away from the landing towards the flat area where the impact will be much harder. It's best to take off straight from the lip, then adjust the angle of your board while in the air." — [SA, How to hit a frontside hip](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/news/how-to-hit-a-frontside-hip-on-a-snowboard); [SA, How to hit a backside hip](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/news/how-to-hit-a-backside-hip-on-a-snowboard)
- SA says the same applies more strongly as jumps get bigger: "As you go bigger, you need to takeoff with a very flat base and leave the lip in a straight direction. Wait until you're in the air to adjust your board for the landing." — [SA, frontside hip](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-hit-a-frontside-hip-on-a-snowboard)
- SA's backside hip page says the edge alone can redirect the exit: "Using your toe edge too much can shoot you into the flat spot of the landing." — [SA, backside hip](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-hit-a-backside-hip-on-a-snowboard)
- Anecdotal (forum, BOARDWORLD archive, snippet only): a poster describes two ways to get a spin off a jump. One is going straight at the jump with the shoulders and head pre-rotated. The other is "taking a heel edge and hitting the jump at a slight angle" with a twisted upper body. — [BOARDWORLD forum reply](https://boardworld.com.au/forums/viewreply/32528) (fetch blocked; snippet only)
- Side hits are a different case: "When you think about spinning off a side hit, you are naturally put on edge as you approach it. Park jumps, on the other hand usually have a straight approach and require proper set-up." — [SA, Entry paths for spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- SA describes the backside 180 entry as "going wide and coming in on the heel edge, then shifting to the toe edge as you reach the jump". It stresses that "the angle of the entry path is the most important thing". This is an angled *approach*, not an angled *exit*: the takeoff is on the toe edge "straight off the jump". — [SA, Improve your backside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/17257876-improve-your-backside-180s); [SA, How to backside 180 on park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/how-to-backside-180-on-park-jumps)
- Conflicting or unclear: one search summary said SA's frontside 180 guide prefers "taking off the lip with the board across the jump's line" to reach the centre of the landing. The phrase was not shown verbatim and could not be checked. It may describe the edge or upper-body position rather than heading. Treat it as **unverified**. — [SA, Ultimate guide to frontside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/16927500-the-ultimate-guide-to-frontside-180s-on-a-snowboard)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] "Hitting it at a slight angle" does happen in the real world, mostly on small jumps and 180s, where a little pre-turn on the lip starts the rotation. Coaches still class a visible angle as a fault on bigger jumps.
- [INFERENCE] The designer's "not always from the centre" is true of real riders, but as **variance from a centred target**, not as a choice of line.

### Gaps
- No source describes a deliberate side-of-lip takeoff on a standard straight kicker by advanced riders, such as using one corner of a wide big-air jump for a specific trick. I could not confirm or rule this out.
- No AASI/CASI/BASI manual text found.

## Q2. How wide are kickers compared with the board's path? How much lateral freedom is there? Do coaches say "aim for the middle"?

### Takeaway
FIS sets a **minimum Big Air kicker width of 5.0 m**. A board is about 0.25–0.3 m wide, so the lateral room is large. Coaching wording is "straight path ... to the middle of the landing" rather than "middle of the lip". Park safety codes I found do not use a "middle of the jump" rule.

### Cited Findings
- FIS Big Air technical data: kicker width JW "min. 5.0 m", kicker height "min. 2.0 m", take-off angle "min. 25.0°", drop-in ramp width "min. 5.0 m". Source is the 2023/24 Southern Hemisphere precisions document; newer rulebooks may differ. — [FIS Precisions SBFSFK SH 23/24 (PDF)](https://assets.fis-ski.com/image/upload/fis-prod/assets/Precisions_SBFSFK_Southern_Hemisphere_2324.pdf)
- For contrast, aerials kickers are much narrower, about 1.2–1.5 m wide at the top (search summary of FIS aerials explainer). — [FIS, Aerials 101](https://www.fis-ski.com/freestyle/news/moguls-aerials-2025-26/aerials-101-understanding-aerials-and-aerials-mixed-team)
- Coaching target (search summary, attributed to one of the SA/Whitelines/Instructables 360 guides; the exact page was not identified): "Your board should be facing perpendicular to the lip of the jump upon takeoff. This will ensure a straight path in the air to the middle of the landing." — candidate pages: [Whitelines FS 360 Ben Kilner](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-fs-360-ben-kilner.html), [SA How to frontside 360 on park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/how-to-frontside-360-on-park-jumps), [Instructables BS 360](https://www.instructables.com/How-to-Backside-360-on-a-Snowboard/) (attribution uncertain)
- The ASTM standard defines the lip as the end of the takeoff transition, where the surface angle departs sharply from the takeoff angle. It does not prescribe a lateral line. — [ASTM F3237-23 sample (PDF)](https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/115931/ae16bd248954456a9c280753c068db3a/ASTM-F3237-23.pdf)
- Park etiquette sources (REI, Snowboarding Days, Burton, Blue Tomato) talk about checking the feature is clear, not joining a line mid-feature, and not stopping in landings. None found says "use the middle of the jump". — [Snowboarding Days, Shretiquette](https://www.snowboardingdays.com/?p=1663); [REI, Terrain park etiquette](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/terrain-park-etiquette.html)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE / DERIVATION] On a 5 m kicker, a board about 0.3 m wide has roughly ±2.3 m of lateral room before its edge reaches the side of the lip. So a takeoff point anywhere in the central ~3 m is physically "on the kicker". What matters for safety is the **heading**: the landing is usually about as wide as the kicker, and a few degrees of heading error over a 15–25 m flight moves the touchdown by 1–2+ m (e.g. 5° × 20 m ≈ 1.7 m).
- [INFERENCE] Lateral position on the lip matters less than exit heading, because a lateral offset of x only moves the landing by x, while a heading error grows with flight distance.

### Gaps
- No measured data on how much riders' takeoff points actually vary across the lip in park or contest.
- No found source states the landing-zone width relative to kicker width for big air.
- The Smart Style / Park Smart wording could not be retrieved.

## Q3. What decides where on the lip, and at what heading, the rider leaves? Is there a link to set-up carve timing?

### Takeaway
Sources agree that **approach line + set-up carve timing and size + edge use on the transition** set the exit heading. Early or excessive carving puts some of the rotation and heading change on the snow before the lip. The result is a diagonal exit and sideways drift. This is the strongest support for the designer's causal link (set-up timing → exit angle/position). Late-timing effects are described in terms of rotation, not position.

### Cited Findings
- Whitelines (BS 360, snippet): "Carving across the kicker too early drifts you sideways over the landing and may even send you off the side of the jump. The turn and pop should all happen right at the lip." — [Whitelines, BS 360 Ben Kilner](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-bs-360-ben-kilner.html) (also in note 04)
- Turning or spinning too early up the face: "The typical results are reduced speed (knuckle landing) and drifting off the landing." — search summary attributed to SA's "Top 3 problems ... frontside 360" page. — [SA, Top 3 problems FS 360](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/16098668-top-3-problems-when-learning-how-to-frontside-360-on-a-snowboard)
- Setting up too early means "not going in a straight direction, and you will have made some of your rotation even before you leave the jump". The fix: "you should only be shifting to your toe edge as you are rolling up the lip of the jump". — [SA, Improve your backside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/17257876-improve-your-backside-180s)
- Carving too far: "you'll end up turning too much in the lip". Riders end up "already spinning some of the 360 and 'scrubbing' off the lip of the jump". — [SA, How to spin 360s, 540s & 720s on large jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps)
- The posture at takeoff also turns the heading: leaning back into a heel-edge carve at takeoff makes you "drift sideways in the air and go slightly inverted" (Whitelines FS 360 snippet). — [Whitelines, FS 360 Ben Kilner](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-fs-360-ben-kilner.html)
- A teaching manual (NZSIA snippet) lists "Student drifts off the side before reaching the end of the feature" as a fault. The fixes are body alignment and "avoiding last-minute speed checks" on the approach. So a late speed check is another cause of lateral deviation. — [NZSIA learning](https://learning.nzsia.org/?p=14823)
- SA's backside 180 guide treats the entry path angle as the key variable: "the angle of the entry path is the most important thing". — [SA, Improve your backside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/17257876-improve-your-backside-180s)
- Lip shape: homemade-kicker guidance links lip *sharpness* to pop. It says a moderate lip is better for rotations. Nothing found on asymmetric or turned lips throwing riders sideways. — [Think Empire, How to build a perfect kicker](https://thinkempire.com/blogs/news/how-to-build-a-perfect-snowboarding-kicker)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Mechanism consistent with the sources and with note 07:
  - The set-up S-line is meant to end with the board parallel to the kicker axis at the set-up point.
  - If the second (takeoff-edge) arc starts **too early** or is **too tight**, the board is still turning as it climbs the transition. It leaves with a heading rotated toward the spin direction (FS heel → rider's heel side; BS toe → toe side for a regular rider) and slightly to that side of the centreline.
  - If the second arc starts **too late**, the rider has not yet returned to parallel. They may leave from the side the first arc carried them to, still heading slightly back across. No source states this late-case geometry explicitly.
- [INFERENCE] For a game: a model where lateral position and heading at the lip = f(set-up start time vs ideal, carve intensity) matches the coaching cause-and-effect. The ideal timing should yield a centred, square exit, and errors should be symmetric-ish around it. Coaches describe the "early" error much more often than the "late" one.

### Gaps
- No quantitative link (metres or degrees per unit of timing error) in any source.
- No source on "late set-up" giving an exit offset on the opposite side.
- No source on lips deliberately built angled or turned (other than hips, where the *landing* is turned, not the lip).

## Q4. What happens after a side or angled takeoff?

### Takeaway
Reported consequences: lateral drift over the landing, going "off the side of the jump", landing in the flat or knuckle (on hips: shot "towards the flat area where the impact will be much harder"), speed loss and knuckle landings, and edge-catch risk. Judges penalise landings that are not clean.

### Cited Findings
- Off the side of the jump / sideways drift over the landing. — [Whitelines BS 360](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-bs-360-ben-kilner.html)
- Knuckle plus drift off the landing, with worst case "toe-edge catch" (search summary). — [SA, Top 3 problems FS 360](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/16098668-top-3-problems-when-learning-how-to-frontside-360-on-a-snowboard)
- On hips: shot away from the landing into the flat; harder impact. — [SA, frontside hip](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/news/how-to-hit-a-frontside-hip-on-a-snowboard)
- Mitigation: aim "to land a little more on your tail so if the board is a few degrees off it'll right itself" (Whitelines FS 360, snippet). — [Whitelines FS 360](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-fs-360-ben-kilner.html)
- Judging: execution covers "smooth landing", and X Games lists landings among its criteria. — [Wikipedia, Slopestyle](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Slopestyle); [X Games women's slopestyle 2024](https://xgames.com/events/x-games-aspen-2024/competitions/womens-snowboard-slopestyle)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] No found source says the edge of the lip "falls away" or that rolled lip sides throw a rider. Physically, a kicker's side walls slope down. A rider whose edge crosses the lip near the side would get uneven support, but this is not sourced.

### Gaps
- No sourced description of the lip's lateral shape (rolled edges) affecting the takeoff.
