## 1. Chronology of rotation records (men & women), max degrees/inversions, hardest directions

### Takeaway
- **Men's spin record in competition:** 2340 (6.5 rotations). Hiroto Ogiwara (JPN) landed it as a backside 2340 at X Games Aspen in January 2025, and landed it again to win X Games Aspen 2026.
- **Most inversions:** the backside 2160 he landed off-contest at The Nines in April 2022 is often called a "quint cork" (5 flips). That claim is disputed.
- **Women's frontier:** triple-cork 1440s are now routine at the top. Kokomo Murase reportedly landed the first women's 1620 triple cork in competition at X Games Aspen in January 2026; this is reported by one source and contradicted by another.
- **Hardest direction:** coaches generally rate switch backside the hardest.

### Cited Findings: hardest directions
- **Switch backside** is described as "the most difficult spin direction for most riders". — [Snowboard Addiction terminology](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/news/snowboard-terminology) (snippet)
- **Frontside** is generally easier than backside because the second half of the spin faces uphill. Backside spins land partly blind unless the rider finishes early. — [Snowboard Addiction](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/snowboard-terminology) (snippet)
- **Cab** means switch frontside. It is relatively easier because the rider can see the landing for the whole trick. — [Snowboard Addiction](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-switch-180-on-a-snowboard) (snippet)
- In Olympic big air and slopestyle, riders must spin different ways across their counting tricks. — [NBC, "riding switch hidden skill", 2026](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/olympics/2026-milan-cortina/snowboarding-riding-switch-hidden-skill/4022680/?amp=1) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Spin record by year:

  | Spin | Year | Rider |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1440 | 2010–11 | Horgmo |
  | 1620 | 2015 | Kadono |
  | 1800 (quad) | 2015 | Morgan |
  | 1980 in competition | about 2022 | Rinnekangas |
  | 2160 | 2022 | Ogiwara |
  | 2340 in competition | 2025 | Ogiwara |

- The spin record has grown by 180° about every 2 to 3 years. As of Nov 2026 I found no report of a 2520 by anyone.
- **Inversions:** the cork count is capped at about 4 widely accepted (quad), with a contested 5 (Ogiwara's 2160). Big rotations are built from off-axis corks; flat-spin and on-axis records were not found.
- **Women** are about 8 to 10 years behind men: triple 1440s are standard in 2025–26, and a 1620 is claimed in 2026.

## 3. FIS / World Snowboard judging criteria and scoring

### Takeaway
- Slopestyle and big air are scored as an overall impression from 0 to 100.
- Media list five criteria: difficulty, execution, amplitude, variety, progression (often shortened to "DEAL"/"DAVE-P"); slopestyle also scores composition and flow.
- A panel of 6 judges is used; the highest and lowest scores are dropped and the remaining 4 averaged.
- Olympic big air: 3 runs, and the best 2 count only if they are different tricks / spin directions.
- Landing faults are scored by deduction bands, from 1–9 points for hand drags up to about 30 for body-first impact.

### Cited Findings
- **Big air criteria:** "overall impression based on execution, difficulty, amplitude, variety and progression". — [JHSE 18(3) paper, Univ. Alicante](https://rua.ua.es/dspace/bitstream/10045/132281/6/JHSE_18-3_01.pdf) (snippet)
- **"DEAL":** PyeongChang 2018 official site described six judges scoring amplitude, execution, difficulty, progression and landing. — [Heavy, 2018-02](https://heavy.com/sports/2018/02/snowboard-big-air-rules-judging-scoring-what-is/)
- **Scale and panel:** 0–100 scale; six judges; highest and lowest dropped; four averaged. — [LEX18 "Snowboarding 101: Scoring", 2026](https://www.lex18.com/news/snowboarding-101-scoring)
- **Olympic format:** a rider's counted scores must come from different tricks / directions. ESPN: scores "must come from a different trick for each of their runs". — [ESPN explainer, 2026](https://africa.espn.com/olympics/story/_/id/47707150/how-does-olympic-snowboarding-work-rules-scoring-more); [NBC switch article](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/olympics/2026-milan-cortina/snowboarding-riding-switch-hidden-skill/4022680/?amp=1)
- **Progression (FIS Judging Handbook):** "a trick, grab, or anything new or creative for the level of competition". — [FIS SB/FK Judges Handbook, Fall 2023](https://assets.fis-ski.com/f/252177/298852da13/fis_sb_fk-judgeshandbook_clean-fall-2023.pdf) (snippet only)
- **Deduction bands (FIS handbook):** "1–9 small mistakes on landings including: hand drag, small hand-touch". The spring 2022 update lists hand drag and butt check as "major deductions" in some sections, so its category varies by section. — [FIS SB/FK Judges Handbook update, spring 2022](https://assets.fis-ski.com/f/252177/7c81eac52f/fis_sb_fk-judgeshandbook_update_spring-2022.pdf) (snippet)
- **2020–21 FIS big air deduction ranges** (unofficial third-party summary):

  | Fault | Deduction |
  |---|---|
  | Hand touch, drag, off balance | 1–9 |
  | Two hands down, or reversing the trick (e.g. sliding out / turning back on landing) | 10–19 |
  | Major body contact | 20–29 |
  | Body hits snow first | 30 |

  Source: [Marcello Centurione's Pocket Guide, BC Snowboard](https://www.bcsnowboard.com/wp-content/uploads/MC-Pocket-Guide-to-SB-Freestyle-Judging-2020-21.pdf) (snippet)
- **Run-score bands in the handbook (slopestyle-type runs):**
  - Crash in the first half of the course: 10–20.
  - Crash at the end of an otherwise good run: 20–30.
  - Below-average runs: 30–45.
  - Source: [FIS handbook snippets](https://www.freestylesnowsports.co.uk/+documents/Judge%20Training/FIS%20Judges%20Manual.pdf)
- **Clean vs fought rotation:** the 2016 IJC judge manual asks judges "Is the rotation clean or fought?", so over- or under-rotation, and correcting it on landing, are execution issues. — [IJC Judge Manual 2016, Canada Snowboard](https://www.canadasnowboard.ca/files/Event%20documents/Event%20PTSA%20documents/2016_IJC_Judge_Manual.pdf) (snippet)
- **Comparison, freestyle skiing aerials** (a different discipline, Nov 2026 handbook): any hand contact caps the landing score at 2.0, unless the contact is momentary. Not applicable to snowboard. — [FIS Freestyle Skiing Judging Handbook, Nov 2026](https://assets.fis-ski.com/f/252177/x/40158d23e4/freestyle-skiing-judging-handbook.pdf) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **Grabs:** they are part of difficulty and execution. A held, tweaked grab adds to the score, and an unusual grab counts as progression. No numeric grab bonus exists, because judging is by overall impression, not a code of points. Not directly confirmed by fetched text.
- **Under-rotation:** a short landing (board not aligned, skid or "reverse") falls in the 10–19 band; a slight over/under rotation corrected on landing is about 1–9. This follows from the bands above.
- **Game scoring model:**
  1. Base score from difficulty: degrees, corks, direction (switch BS > BS > cab/switch FS > FS), and grab.
  2. Multiply or adjust for amplitude.
  3. Subtract landing deductions.
  4. Big air: take the best two scores with different directions.

### Gaps
- The current FIS handbook (2025–26 edition) text, exact criterion weights and any formal direction rule could not be read; assets.fis-ski.com was blocked.
- The "different directions" rule is confirmed only through media wording.
- No source gave exact score ranges for a crash in big air for the 2025–26 rules.

## 4. Practical difficulty ladder and physical limits

### Takeaway
- No authoritative published ladder was found. The ladder below is a domain-knowledge inference anchored on the sourced records above.
- **Jump size:** Olympic big air ramps are about 40–49 m tall, with flights over 36 m.
- **Speed:** the only big air takeoff speed figure found is about 64 km/h (40 mph), at Fenway 2016.
- No official airtime figures exist.

### Inferences: physics (my own projectile estimates, not sourced)
- **Takeoff speed:** about 18–22 m/s (65–80 km/h), consistent with the 64 km/h Fenway figure.
- **Airtime:** with a kicker angle of about 30–35° and a landing well below the lip, airtime is about 3 s.
- **Spin rates this implies:**
  - 2340 (6.5 rev): about 2.2 rev/s.
  - 1980 (5.5 rev): about 1.8 rev/s.
  - 1440 (4 rev): about 1.3 rev/s.
- **Why higher rotations are rare:**
  1. Spin rate is limited by the angular momentum generated at takeoff, with no further input in the air. Riders can only speed up by tucking, which shrinks the moment of inertia.
  2. Every extra 180° needs about 0.25 s more airtime or a faster spin.
  3. Landing alignment errors grow with the spin rate.
  4. Few jumps are big enough; record tricks happen at X Games Aspen or session events like The Nines with custom jumps.
  5. Olympic and World Cup formats require two directions, so riders pick a slightly smaller trick in their weaker direction.
