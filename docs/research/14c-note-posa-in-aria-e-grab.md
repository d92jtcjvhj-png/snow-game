# In-air posture, grabs, tweaks, opening up and spotting the landing (big air / slopestyle)

Reference: **regular stance (left foot forward)**. Front hand = left hand, rear hand = right hand. Toe edge is on the rider's chest side, heel edge on the back side. **Goofy mirrors everything**: swap left and right (front hand = right hand). The grab name does not change, because names are defined by front/rear hand and toe/heel edge, not by left/right.

## 1. Basic compact air pose (knees to chest, board to hands, trunk)

### Takeaway
Coaching sources agree: pop, then pull the knees up toward the chest so the board comes up to a hand that is already waiting; do not reach down. Arms stay calm, shoulders stay stacked over the board, head up. The tuck also lowers the moment of inertia (in gymnastics, tuck is about 3-5x smaller than a straight body), which speeds up rotation.

### Cited Findings
- Pull the knees up first; reach for the board only once the knees are near the chest. It is easier to bring the board up to you than to drop the hand down to it — [Ridestore Mag, How to do snowboard grabs](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-do-snowboard-grabs/); [Snowboard Addiction, How to 360 with grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/en-eu/blogs/training-education/how-to-360-with-grabs-on-a-snowboard)
- Reaching down with the arms can throw off your axis. Keep the arms quiet, and bring knees and board up to a hand already in position — [Snowboarding Forum, Grabs and flexibility?](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/grabs-and-flexibility.22416/) (forum, anecdotal)
- Stay compact in the air with knees pulled up toward the chest. A strong pop makes it easier to get compact — [Abom, How to jump on a snowboard](https://abom.com/how-to-jump-on-a-snowboard/)
- "keep our shoulders in line over our snowboard to stay straight in the air"; do not straighten the legs too early — [Shred School / Ed Shreds](https://www.edshreds.com/how-to-jump-on-a-snowboard/)
- Nose grab: keep the upper body neutral and the head up, not bent down toward the board — [Snowboard Addiction, How to Nose & Tail Grab](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-nose-tail-grab-on-a-snowboard); [Progresspedia, Nose grab](https://progresspedia.org/courses/snowboarding/nose-grab/)
- Moment of inertia about the somersault axis (adult male): straight 19.8 kg·m², piked 5.9, tucked 3.8, so the straight-to-tuck ratio is about 5.2 — [U. Toronto, Physics of Somersaulting and Twisting](https://www.physics.utoronto.ca/~phy189h1/Physics%20of%20Somersaults%20and%20Twists.pdf)
- Diving estimates: standing 11.3 (12.3 in Whittsett) kg·m², tuck 3.4 (4.7), pike about 5.6-5.8 kg·m² — [Splashing physics of springboard diving, IOP 2024](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1361-6552/ad9fcb)
- Pulling the limbs in speeds up rotation, because angular momentum stays nearly constant. The feet are strapped to a board, so cat-style twisting does not transfer directly — [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/olympic-big-air-snowboarders-use-physics-to-their-advantage/)
- Grabbing and tweaking while spinning is a way to change the moment of inertia and control rotation speed — [CASI Reference Guide](https://www.instructor-academy.com/uploads/downloads/CASI_RefGuide.pdf)
- In big-air takeoffs (11 Chinese national-team riders, BS 720 to 1800), the moment of inertia **at takeoff** went up with trick difficulty. Riders are more extended at the pop and only compact afterward. Separately, 157 corks by 15 riders showed angular velocity, takeoff speed and moment of inertia to be the key indicators of rotation amount — [Applied Sciences 2025, 15(12):6618](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/12/6618)

### Inferences
- Pose sequence: extended at the pop (legs almost straight, arms up or wound) → compact within about the first 15-25 % of air time (**ESTIMATE**) → hold → open before landing.
- **ESTIMATE**, compact grab pose for a rider with the board strapped on (the board limits the tuck compared with gymnastics):
  - knee flexion 100-130° (0° = straight);
  - hip flexion 80-110°, thighs toward the chest, not touching it;
  - ankles in dorsiflexion as set by the binding highbacks, about 15-20°;
  - trunk leaning forward 20-35° from the board normal, back rounded slightly, shoulders over the board;
  - head up, neck neutral to slightly extended, eyes ahead or on the landing, not on the board.
  - Grabbing arm: shoulder flexion 30-60° down/forward, elbow 20-60° (fairly straight; the board comes up to it).
- Gymnastic tuck (for flips) goes deeper: **ESTIMATE** knees about 130-150°, hips about 120-140°.
- Physics hook: use the tuck/pike/straight inertia ratios (about 1 : 1.5 : 3-5) for ω = L / I(pose). The project already treats the grab as the compact pose and open as slow.

### Gaps
- No measured snowboard in-flight joint angles found; a motion-capture paper exists (IMU vs markerless, ResearchGate 390411706), but its contents were not reachable.
- No source gave the time from pop to full tuck.

## 2. Grab definitions (regular: front hand = left, rear hand = right)

### Takeaway
The four basic grabs map to front/rear hand × toe/heel edge, between the bindings: Mute (front, toe), Indy (rear, toe), Melon (front, heel), Stalefish (rear, heel). Nose and tail grabs are taken at the tips. "Bad" grabs (Tindy, Tailfish) are rear-hand grabs taken behind the rear binding instead of between the feet.

### Cited Findings
- **Indy**: rear hand on the toe edge between the bindings. It is often the first grab learned — [NBC Olympics glossary](https://www.nbcolympics.com/news/snowboarding-101-glossary); [Snowboard Addiction, Basic grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/basic-grabs-on-a-snowboard)
- **Mute**: front hand on the toe edge **between the bindings** (mirror of the Indy). It can be "boned" by extending the back leg — [NBC Olympics glossary](https://www.nbcolympics.com/news/snowboarding-101-glossary); [Snowboard Addiction, Complicated World of Grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/112716804-the-complicated-world-of-snowboard-grabs)
- **Melon**: front hand on the heel edge between the feet. **Stalefish**: rear hand on the heel edge between the feet (rear hand reaches behind the rear leg) — [Snowboard Addiction, Basic grabs](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/grabs/basic-grabs-on-a-snowboard)
- **Method**: a tweaked melon. Front hand on the heel edge between the feet, then "tweak the board up behind you and arch your hips forward". Another guide says: "Grab the board with your front hand and extend the rear leg". The "proper" method is debated — [Red Bull infographic](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/learning-snowboard-tricks-the-easy-way-infographic); [Snowboarding Profiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions); [Wikipedia, Method grab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Method_grab)
- Method sequence: hold, then "Spot your landing, release the grab, and orient your body for touch down" — [Snowboarder Mag, Method air](https://www.snowboarder.com/how-to/master-method-air-like-a-pro-step-by-step-guide)
- **Nose grab**: front hand on the nose; the very tip looks best. The legs do the work: front knee up, back leg extended down, and the nose comes to the hand — [Snowboard Addiction, Nose & Tail](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-nose-tail-grab-on-a-snowboard); [Progresspedia](https://progresspedia.org/courses/snowboarding/nose-grab/)
- **Tail grab**: rear hand on the tail. If it is hard to reach, bend the rear leg more than the front. For style, stretch the front leg out before releasing — [Whitelines, How to tail grab](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-tail-grab.html); [Snowboard Addiction](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-nose-tail-grab)
- **Seatbelt**: front hand reaches across the body to the tail. **Rocket air**: front hand on the nose and rear hand on the tail — [mataceinc, 10 essential grabs](https://mataceinc.com/blogs/news/10-essential-snowboard-grabs-every-rider-should-know)
- **Japan**: sources disagree. One version is the front hand on the toe edge between the bindings while the front knee is pulled down to the board. Another is "a super tweaked out Mute grab, but grabbed behind your back". A third has the hand reaching through to the heel edge — [Snowboarding Days list](https://www.snowboardingdays.com/snowboarding-trick-names-list/); [InTheSnow glossary](https://www.inthesnow.com/guide-snowboarding-terminology/)
- **Tindy**: rear hand on the toe edge between the rear binding and the tail. "The ugly brother of the Indy… poor form". **Tailfish**: rear hand on the heel edge between the rear binding and the tail ("bad stalefish") — [Snowboarding Profiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions); [Snowboard Addiction](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/112716804-the-complicated-world-of-snowboard-grabs)
- **Crail**: rear hand on the nose or on the toe edge in front of the front foot, with the rear leg boned. Some riders call any rear-hand toe-side grab in front of the front foot a crail — [Snowboarding Profiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions)

### Inferences
- **The Mute is between the feet.** A front-hand toe-side grab **in front of the front foot** is usually called a nose-mute or "mute nose" (**ESTIMATE**: naming convention, not found in the sources).
- Animation summary (regular, **ESTIMATE**s for the arm/free arm):

| Grab | Grabbing hand → spot | Body shape | Free arm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mute | L hand → toe edge between bindings, around the front binding/center | knees up; chest over the board, slight forward reach across the body | R arm out back/up, elbow 20-40°, about shoulder height |
| Indy | R hand → toe edge between bindings, near the rear binding | knees up, hips square, R shoulder drops slightly | L arm out front/up toward the nose, roughly pointing at the landing |
| Melon | L hand → heel edge between feet, reaches down behind the front leg | front knee drops, hips pushed back | R arm up/back |
| Stalefish | R hand → heel edge between feet, behind the rear leg | rear knee up and out, trunk turned toward the tail | L arm forward/up |
| Method | L hand → heel edge; board pulled up behind the back | rear leg boned/extended, hips arched forward, board almost vertical behind the rider, back arched | R arm high/back, open, "style" arm |
| Nose | L hand → nose tip | front knee to the chest, rear leg extended down (board tilted nose-up) | R arm back for balance |
| Tail | R hand → tail | rear knee to the chest, front leg extended (board tilted tail-up) | L arm forward/up |
| Japan | L hand → toe edge between bindings, board pulled behind the back | front knee driven down toward the board, board tweaked behind | R arm up/back |
| Tindy / Tailfish | R hand → toe/heel edge behind the rear binding | looks reached-for; board not brought up | — (show as sloppy) |
| Crail | R hand → toe edge in front of the front foot / nose | rear leg boned | L arm free, up/back |

- Free-arm rule (**ESTIMATE** from coaching cues): calm, away from the body, roughly opposite to the grabbing hand, at or above shoulder height. Flailing reads as loss of control.

### Gaps
- No source specified where the free arm goes for each grab. The table is an animator estimate.
- The Japan definition is disputed; the most common modern use is the front hand on the toe edge, board pulled behind with the front knee down (**ESTIMATE**).

## 3. Tweaks and pokes

### Takeaway
A tweak is stretching the body or board while grabbing; to "bone" is to straighten a leg. A tail poke means the rear leg is straightened and the tail pushed away; a nose poke means the front leg is straightened and the nose pushed away. Judges reward tweaks and pokes.

### Cited Findings
- Tweak: "Stretching the body to accentuate or exaggerate a grab". Bone: to straighten a leg — [REI glossary](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/snowboarding-glossary.html); [Canada Snowboard terms](https://www.canadasnowboard.ca/en/about/snowboarding/)
- Tail poke: the athlete straightens the rear leg and "pokes" the tail away from the body. Nose poke: front leg boned and nose poked away, usually while grabbing it — [CBC glossary](https://www.cbc.ca/sports/2.722/snowboard-glossary-1.854390); [InTheSnow](https://www.inthesnow.com/guide-snowboarding-terminology/)
- Mute "boned" = back leg extended; Method = front-hand heel grab plus rear leg extended — [Snowboard Addiction](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/112716804-the-complicated-world-of-snowboard-grabs); [Snowboarding Profiles](https://snowboardingprofiles.com/snowboards-tricks-definitions)

### Inferences (ESTIMATE)
- **Indy tweak**: R hand pulls the toe edge toward the chest/up, the front leg extends (knee about 20-40°), the rear knee stays bent (about 110°). The board tilts nose-down and away, toward the heel side.
- **Tail poke**: tail grab (or no grab), rear knee about 10-30° (nearly straight), front knee about 110-130°. The board pitches so the tail points down and back.
- **Nose poke**: mirror of the tail poke. Front knee about 10-30°, rear knee bent, nose pushed down and forward.
- **Method tweak**: rear knee about 20-40°, front knee about 120°, hips pushed forward (hip extension about 0-10°), back arched. Board pulled up behind toward vertical, roughly at shoulder height.
- Timing: the tweak peaks around the apex and is held for about 0.2-0.5 s (**ESTIMATE**).

### Gaps
- No numeric definition of how much a leg must straighten to count as "boned".

## 4. Grabs during spins and corks; head direction

### Takeaway
Riders set the rotation at the takeoff (head and shoulders lead), then bring the board to the hand. On corks they look over and slightly down toward the leading shoulder, and some riders watch the spot they are grabbing until they have it. Grabbing speeds up rotation (tuck), and holding a tweak can slow it.

### Cited Findings
- The head and shoulders rotate first. Lock the eyes on the landing rather than the takeoff; looking at the takeoff tends to stop the spin — [Snowboarding Forum, Tips on a 360](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/tips-on-a-360-video.53257/)
- FS 360 tramp tutorial: chin high, looking just above the shoulder in the spin direction — [Snowboard Addiction, FS 360](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-360)
- Keep the body compact, use the arms to control rotation, and look over the leading shoulder to track the landing. Not spotting early is a common mistake — [Skadi Snowsports, FS 360](https://skadisnowsports.com/how-to-do-a-frontside-360/)
- Cork 720: look over and slightly down to the shoulder in the spin direction. Dropping the head toward the shoulder after the rotation starts gives a clean cork. The head ends level with the feet, with a slight shoulder dip — [Newschoolers, How to cork 720](https://www.newschoolers.com/forum/thread/774879/How-to-Cork-720) (ski forum, anecdotal)
- Set the full rotation first, then actively spot the tail with your eyes until you have the grab — [Newschoolers video, Cork 720 blunt](https://www.newschoolers.com/videos/watch/983491/HOW-TO-CORK-720-BLUNT-GRAB-on-Skis-) (ski)
- Reach for a grab, or the rotation goes by too slowly — [Newschoolers, Cork 720 help](https://www.newschoolers.com/threads/cork-720-help.812913/)
- Grab plus tweak while spinning changes the moment of inertia and rotation speed — [CASI guide](https://www.instructor-academy.com/uploads/downloads/CASI_RefGuide.pdf)

### Inferences (ESTIMATE)
- Head yaw leads the body by about 30-60° in the first half of a spin, then the head spots the landing while the hips are still turning.
- On corks the head tilts toward the leading shoulder (lateral neck flexion about 15-30°); the gaze line roughly follows the off-axis tilt.
- The grab is taken after the rotation is set (about 0.1-0.2 s after the pop) and held through the middle of the spin.

### Gaps
- No snowboard-specific coaching source on head timing for corks; only ski forums.

## 5. Opening up before landing; spotting

### Takeaway
Sequence: spot the landing → release the grab → extend the legs just before touchdown → absorb with bent knees, board flat. Sources give no exact fraction of air time or degrees; spotting happens "just past halfway" in flips and in the last quarter of a 360 (**ESTIMATE**).

### Cited Findings
- "Spot your landing, release the grab, and orient your body for touch down. Keep your knees loose to absorb impact" — [Snowboarder Mag](https://www.snowboarder.com/how-to/master-method-air-like-a-pro-step-by-step-guide)
- "Before landing, extend your legs, and when you land, absorb the compression by bending your knees again" — [Abom](https://abom.com/how-to-jump-on-a-snowboard/)
- Extend slightly just before landing, then bend on impact; do not straighten too early — [Shred School](https://www.edshreds.com/how-to-jump-on-a-snowboard/)
- Straight air: base flat, knees bent, spot the landing, land flat with knees bent — [Burton blog](https://www.burton.com/en-us/blogs/the-burton-blog/snowboard-tricks-to-learn)
- Dropping the gaze to the fall line too early makes riders open up and stall around 90° — [Snowboarding Forum, Help with 360s](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/help-with-360s.37420/)
- Judges reward grabs held as long as possible — [Sportsgoogly](https://www.sportsgoogly.com/snowboard-judging-explained/); [Snowboard International](https://www.snowboard.international/features/how-does-snowboard-big-air-work-at-the-winter-olympics/)

### Inferences (ESTIMATE)
- Release the grab at about 70-85 % of air time (with about 0.2-0.4 s left). In spins, release about 60-120° before the final orientation.
- Spot the landing around 270° in a 360, around 630° in a 720, and so on (last quarter turn).
- Opening: arms go out to the sides/forward at about 45-80° of shoulder abduction, knees to about 20-40° at contact, hips about 30-50°, trunk upright over the board.
- At contact the legs absorb to knees about 70-100°.

### Gaps
- No source gives a measured release timing.

## 6. Straight air and flips

### Takeaway
Straight air: board flat, knees bent, shoulders over the board, calm arms, eyes on the landing. Backflip: pop and extend, throw the arms up and back, then tuck (knees to chest); tuck to speed up, extend to slow down; spot the landing just past halfway, arms down only right before landing. A frontflip rotates toward the nose and needs a bit more air because the rotation is slower.

### Cited Findings
- Backflip: pop and extend the knees, lean back at the lip, timing right at the takeoff point — [Skadi, How to backflip on a snowboard](https://skadisnowsports.com/how-to-backflip-on-a-snowboard/)
- "tuck to speed up, extend to slow down, … keep those tips up" — [Pushdalimit, Backflip on skis](https://pushdalimit.com/tutorial/how-to-backflip-on-skis/)
- Stretch both arms toward the sky once weightless to sense the spin direction; bring them down only right before landing. Spot the landing "Just past the halfway mark" — [campingtourist, backflip tips](https://www.campingtourist.com/camping-activities/snowboarding/tips-to-backflip-during-snowboarding/) (blog, lower quality)
- Land with the board flat, knees and hips flexed — [Skadi backflip](https://skadisnowsports.com/how-to-backflip-on-a-snowboard/)
- Frontflip: rotates toward the front foot and needs more air than a backflip because rotation is slower — [Wikipedia, Aerial (skateboarding)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_(skateboarding)) (indirect)
- Straight air: knees bent, base flat — [Burton](https://www.burton.com/en-us/blogs/the-burton-blog/snowboard-tricks-to-learn)

### Inferences (ESTIMATE)
- Straight air: knees 50-80°, hips 40-60°, trunk 10-20° forward, arms relaxed out front/side at about 30-45° abduction with elbows 30-60°, head neutral.
- Backflip in the air: tuck, knees 120-140°, hips 110-130°. Chin neutral to slightly tucked (sources disagree about chin-down versus looking for the landing). Usually a mute or indy grab; open at about 270° of the flip.
- Frontflip: piked/tucked forward, head tucked to the chest at the start, then up to spot the landing.

### Gaps
- No snowboard-specific biomechanics on flip body shape.

## 7. Head and gaze behaviour

### Takeaway
The head leads the rotation, the eyes find the landing as early as possible, and the head stays up (not looking at the board). On corks the head drops toward the leading shoulder.

### Cited Findings
- See sections 4-5 — [Snowboarding Forum](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/tips-on-a-360-video.53257/); [Snowboard Addiction FS 360](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-360); [Skadi](https://skadisnowsports.com/how-to-do-a-frontside-360/); [Newschoolers](https://www.newschoolers.com/forum/thread/774879/How-to-Cork-720)
- Judging: Execution covers takeoff, grabs, air control, flow, style and landing — [FIS Judges Handbook 2019/20](https://www.freestylesnowsports.co.uk/+documents/Judge%20Training/FIS%20Judges%20Manual.pdf). Judges positively cite long grab duration, good grab position and a firmly held board — [Scoring perspectives for big air, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13111472/); [Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1793460/full)

### Inferences (ESTIMATE)
- Implementation: aim the head yaw at the landing with a 0.1-0.15 s lead, clamped to about ±80° from the shoulders. When the landing is behind the rider, look over the leading shoulder.

### Gaps
- No eye-tracking studies for snowboard freestyle were found.
