# Pre-jump carve / set-up turn and lateral drift off kickers (coaching and rider evidence)

Everything below comes from WebSearch result snippets, which are search-engine summaries of those pages. Quoted fragments are what the snippets showed. I found no Reddit or snowboardingforum threads, no AASI/CASI/BASI freestyle manual text, and no contest commentary that explicitly describes lateral drift. ## Q1. Do coaches say that carving too hard or too early makes you take off diagonally or drift sideways?

### Takeaway
Yes. Coaching sources agree on this and say it often. Carving across the kicker too early, or making a full heel-edge carve into the lip, makes the rider leave the lip on a diagonal. They then drift sideways over the landing and can go off the side of the jump. The usual fix is to keep the edge but have the board travelling straight up the kicker, square to the lip, at the moment of takeoff.

### Cited Findings
- Whitelines (general kicker tip, snippet): "Don't start carving across the kicker too early, or you'll drift sideways over the landing and maybe even fall off the side of the jump." It also says the turn and pop should "all happen right at the lip." — [Whitelines BS 360 (Ben Kilner) page / related tip](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-bs-360-ben-kilner.html) (the snippet could not confirm whether this line is Kilner's or from a nearby Whitelines tip; see also [whitelines.com/?p=32748](https://whitelines.com/?p=32748))
- Frontside 360 tip (Whitelines FS 360 / Ski+Board-style tip): "you're aiming to be travelling in a straight line right up to take-off, so resist the instinct to lean back and do a full-on heel edge carve. If you do, you'll drift sideways in the air and go slightly inverted." — [Whitelines FS 360 Ben Kilner](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-fs-360-ben-kilner.html); a similar text appears in [Ski+Board Feb/Mar 2023 tip on Issuu](https://issuu.com/thinkpublishing/docs/skiandboard_febmar_2023/s/20150376). Which snippet this came from is uncertain between the two.
- SA (BS 180 article): you "can leave the jump on a toe-edge but not going in a straight direction off the lip of the jump. This will make your balance worse, and make the gap of the jump longer." The summary also says that setting up too early leads to a non-straight exit and rotation before takeoff. — [SA: How To Improve Your Backside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/17257876-improve-your-backside-180s)
- SA (FS 180 material): a failed set-up carve often makes the rider fly off on a diagonal. The ideal is to take off the lip "with your board perpendicular to the jump" so you land in the sweet spot. — [SA: Ultimate guide to Frontside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/news/16927500-the-ultimate-guide-to-frontside-180s-on-a-snowboard)
- Maverix Snow (Medium) names the "turn/spin too early up the face of the jump" error and gives two results: "reduced speed (knuckle landing) and drifting off the landing." — [Medium: Keys to landing Frontside 180s](https://medium.com/@maverixsnow/the-keys-to-landing-frontside-180-s-on-a-snowboard-1ad6fdcf8ed9)
- An NZSIA coaching resource tells coaches to check that the approach is straight and the takeoff is flat-base. That advice is for straight airs, not spins. — [NZSIA learning](https://learning.nzsia.org/?p=14823)
- Burton (straight airs): "point your board straight, keep your base flat, bend your knees." — [Burton: Snowboard tricks to learn](https://www.burton.com/blogs/the-burton-blog/snowboard-tricks-to-learn)

### Inferences
- The coaching vocabulary is "drift sideways", "drifting off the landing", "not going in a straight direction off the lip", "flying off on a diagonal" and "fall off the side of the jump". I found no well-indexed coaching text using "carving off the lip" or "turning on the lip" as set terms.
- The coaches name two separate mechanisms. (a) Timing: the carve starts too early up the face, so the direction of travel is already diagonal at the lip. (b) Magnitude: a full lean-back edge carve at the lip makes the board's travel curve. Both produce a velocity vector at the lip that is not aligned with the kicker's fall line.
- Too much carve on the face also bleeds speed, which is why coaches pair drift with knuckling.

### Gaps
- No full-text verification was possible because of the network blocks.
- No coaching source gives a number for how many degrees of carve or how much lateral offset is acceptable.

## Q2. Where must the carve end for a straight takeoff, and how do big-spin riders manage their line?

### Takeaway
Consensus: the set-up carve happens before the kicker, on the approach. The rider then rolls onto the takeoff edge as the board climbs the lip, using a small edge angle that comes from the hips and knees, not a big lean. The board still points and travels straight off the lip. I found no sourced rider or coach statement on how 720 to 1440 riders adjust their line, such as setting up wider, aiming at part of the lip, or deliberately leaving across the kicker.

### Cited Findings
- SA entry path for all backside spins: "set up with a smooth heel edge carve towards the side of the jump then transfer to your toe edge while riding up the lip. You will be on your toe edge but create this edge angle by pushing your hips and knees forward rather than leaning over your edge." — [SA: How To Backside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps) (also summarised on [SA: Why your backside 360 isn't working](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/why-your-backside-360-isn-t-working-live-coaching))
- SA, FS 180: "Do one smooth toe edge turn carving roughly in line with the side of the jump. Then, transition to your heel edge for a smooth carve up and off the lip." — [SA: How to Frontside 180 on park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-180-on-park-jumps)
- SA, FS 360: "This heel, toe, heel line of entry allows you to be on your heel edge as you leave the lip of the jump, but still fly in a straight direction while in the air." — [SA: How to Frontside 360 on park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-360-on-park-jumps)
- SA: shift to the toe edge only as you roll up the lip so the board exits straight (BS 180 article summary). — [SA: Improve your Backside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/17257876-improve-your-backside-180s)
- Ridestore: "one smooth toe edge turn just before the jump, which helps you get more pressure on your heel edge," then a transition to the heel edge just before the lip, where you pop off. — [Ridestore: How to rotate on a snowboard](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-rotate-on-a-snowboard/)
- Large jumps: SA has a guide on FS 180 to 720 on large jumps. The snippet only confirmed that spinners pop from an edge because it gives them something to push off. — [SA: How to spin FS 180s-720s on large jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps)
- Elite-level line choice: rider interviews talk about line and trick choice (Kotsenburg, Kadono) and about jump geometry. They say nothing about takeoff angle relative to the lip. — [SI: Kotsenburg](https://www.si.com/edge/2016/02/11/sage-kotsenburg-big-air-fenway-park-x-games-aspen-snowboarding); [SI: Kadono Behind the Trick](https://www.si.com/edge/2016/01/26/yuki-kadono-snowboarding-behind-the-trick-x-games-aspen); [Scientific American: Big Air physics](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/olympic-big-air-snowboarders-use-physics-to-their-advantage/)

### Inferences
- The "carve towards the side of the jump, then switch edge on the lip" pattern means the approach turn is aimed so that its exit lines up with the kicker's fall line by the time the board reaches the transition. The lateral part of the turn should be used up before the lip.
- "Hips and knees forward rather than leaning over your edge" means the takeoff edge is a grip edge for pushing off rotation, not a turning carve. So the edge angle at the lip is small and the board's path stays nearly straight.

### Gaps
- No source found on big-spin (720 to 1440) line management: setting up wider, choosing a side of the lip, or compensating for drift. This would need video breakdowns, coach interviews (Snowboard Pro Camp, national team coaches) or forums, none of which surfaced.
- No source says riders intentionally take off slightly across the kicker. This is unconfirmed.

## Q3. Frontside vs backside differences

### Takeaway
Frontside spins are usually set up from a toe-edge turn and leave from the heel edge. The heel-edge takeoff is where coaches warn about the full lean-back carve that causes sideways drift and inversion, and about over-rotating while stuck on the heels, which makes the board slip out. Backside spins set up on the heel edge and leave from the toe edge. The documented backside error is carving across too early, which causes drift. Edge-catch and wash-out warnings for the frontside mostly concern the landing, not the lip.

### Cited Findings
- SA: frontside spins are naturally set up from the heel edge… (the snippet wording was ambiguous). The same article says the set-up carve adds momentum to the rotation. Its "toe-heel" entry descriptions elsewhere indicate a toe-edge set-up turn then a heel-edge takeoff for frontside. — [SA: Entry paths for spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning); [SA: FS 180](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-180-on-park-jumps)
- SA also teaches frontside spins off the toe edge as an alternative. A glossary defines "off the toes" as a frontside spin started by carving on the toe edge. — [SA: How to spin frontside from the toe edge](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-spin-frontside-from-the-toe-edge)
- FS 360 heel-edge warning: a "full-on heel edge carve" leads to "drift sideways in the air and go slightly inverted." — [Whitelines FS 360 Kilner](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-fs-360-ben-kilner.html) / [Issuu Ski+Board](https://issuu.com/thinkpublishing/docs/skiandboard_febmar_2023/s/20150376)
- CoachUp FS 360: if riders "over power the rotation and they stay over their heels, then the board will generally slip out." It also says to keep legs bent or "you will slip out on your back off the jump." — [CoachUp: Executing a Frontside 360](https://www.coachup.com/nation/?p=781)
- FS 360 landing: "land on your toe edge so you don't wash out on your heels." — [Whitelines FS 360 Tailgrab (Rowan Coultas)](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/school-spin-frontside-rowan-coultas.html)
- REI: a common beginner mistake is leaning onto the heel edge and sliding out. — [REI Beginner tricks](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/beginner-snowboarding-tricks.html)
- Backside: the heel-edge set-up toward the side of the jump, then toe edge on the lip, is described as the ideal path for "all Backside spins." — [SA BS 360](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps)
- Off-axis tilt comes from the shoulder, not the carve: "the more you combine a downwards motion with your lead shoulder into the initiation of your spin, the more off axis you will be in the air." — [SA: How To Backside Cork 540](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-backside-cork-540)
- BS 540: a common error is drifting off to the side of the landing, and the landing is blind. — [SA: How to Backside 540](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-540)

### Inferences
- On a heel-edge takeoff the rider leans back, which makes a deep carve easy, so the frontside heel-edge takeoff is the one most prone to an over-carved, drifting, inverted exit. The phrase "washing out" in the sources mostly refers to landings. No source explicitly describes the lip itself skidding.
- On a toe-edge takeoff the rider pushes hips and knees forward instead of leaning, which is the coaches' explicit guard against over-carving with a backside toe edge.

### Gaps
- No source explicitly says a backside toe-edge takeoff "over-carves" more than a frontside one, or the reverse.

## Q4. What happens when riders over-carve, and how far off-line do they land?

### Takeaway
Coaches list these outcomes: drifting sideways over the landing, landing toward or off the side of the landing ("fall off the side of the jump"), a longer effective gap with more chance of knuckling, worse balance in the air, slight inversion, and edge catches. No source puts a number on the lateral offset. I found no contest commentary that explicitly blames a crash on drift off the takeoff.

### Cited Findings
- "drift sideways over the landing and maybe even fall off the side of the jump." — [Whitelines](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-bs-360-ben-kilner.html)
- A diagonal exit "make[s] your balance worse, and make[s] the gap of the jump longer." — [SA BS 180](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/17257876-improve-your-backside-180s)
- Turning early up the face causes "reduced speed (knuckle landing) and drifting off the landing." — [Medium/Maverix](https://medium.com/@maverixsnow/the-keys-to-landing-frontside-180-s-on-a-snowboard-1ad6fdcf8ed9)
- A flat-base spin takeoff makes the board "slide across the snow and can lead to catching an edge on landing." This is the opposite error from over-carving. — [SA: Entry paths for spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- Contest incidents found, none attributed to lateral drift: Rong Ge (Beijing 2022 big air) went "straight over the finish barrier," which was an overshoot, not drift ([Eurosport](https://www.eurosport.co.uk/snowboard/beijing-2022/2022/oh-straight-over-the-barrier-rong-ge-crashes-celebrates-after-big-air-jump-at-beijing-winter-olympic_sto8790010/story.shtml)). Chris Corning crashed a BS quad cork 1800 attempt in the PyeongChang 2018 final ([Shutterstock editorial](https://secure.shutterstock.com/ja/editorial/image-editorial/chris-corning-us-crashes-on-landing-during-9436434ep)). A 2026 Olympic big air report said Dane Menzies bounced too hard off a 1620 landing (from the search summary only; unverified).

### Inferences
- The kicker's lateral width and the landing width bound the consequences. A small sideways speed held over the airtime (a few seconds on big air) turns into metres of lateral offset. This follows from the physics; no coaching source states it.

### Gaps
- No quantitative offset data and no verified contest commentary on "drift".

## Q5. How much rotation comes from the carve vs the shoulders and upper body?

### Takeaway
Consensus in the coaching material: the set-up carve and takeoff edge give grip and some extra rotational momentum, plus speed control. The rotation itself should be started mainly by the upper body (wind-up and release), with the board leaving straight. SA says not to carve too hard because "you don't want to rely on the set up for your rotation." This supports the idea that a stronger carve trades directional accuracy for rotation.

### Cited Findings
- SA: do not carve too hard in the set-up, because "you don't want to rely on the set up for your rotation." You should still "be using your upper body to initiate the spin." Set-up carves "will also add momentum to your rotation." — [SA: Entry paths for spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- SA: a set-up carve "helps you commit to the speed needed to rotate" and can replace speed checks. — [SA: Entry paths for spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- SA: "you need to use your edges for takeoff to grip from the snow and initiate rotation." An edge takeoff gives the grip to start the spin. — [SA: FS 180](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-180-on-park-jumps)
- Pre-wind: "As you approach the lip, pull it the opposite direction to way you're planning to go during the spin." — [Gian Simmen tip, Coach](https://coachweb.com/exercises/sport-workouts/1752/gian-simmen-snowboard-trick-tip) (attribution from the snippet; it may be from SA)
- Ridestore/other: wind up and release the arms and upper body toward the rotation, start the rotation from the ground up, and don't leave the shoulders behind the board. — [Ridestore: How to rotate](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-rotate-on-a-snowboard/)
- Off-axis rotation (corks) is controlled by the lead shoulder dropping, not by the carve. — [SA BS Cork 540](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-backside-cork-540)

### Inferences
- Coaches model the takeoff as a straight-travelling board on a modest edge, used as a platform, with an upper-body wind-up providing most of the spin. Using a hard carve as the spin source is presented as an error because it costs line and speed.

### Gaps
- No biomechanics study or coach gives a numeric split between carve-generated and upper-body-generated angular momentum. A Jyväskylä thesis (jyx.jyu.fi) appeared in the results but was not accessible for checking.

## Consensus vs anecdote summary
- **Consensus (several independent coaching sources):** leave the lip travelling straight and square, with the set-up carve finished before the lip. Turning or carving across early causes sideways drift, a longer gap or knuckle, and can put you off the side of the landing. Take off on an edge, not flat, but with a modest edge angle. Start the spin mainly with the upper body.
- **Single-source or weaker:** the slight inversion from a FS heel-edge over-carve (one tip); board slip-out from over-powering on the heels (CoachUp).
- **No evidence found:** big-spin line compensation, deliberate cross-kicker takeoffs, numeric offsets, Reddit or forum anecdotes, contest commentary that explicitly cites drift.
