# Set-up S size vs spin size, and early vs late set-up

**Method note.** WebFetch could not reach whitelines.com or snowboardaddiction.com (DNS ENOTFOUND, outside the session's network allowlist; earlier notes 05–08 record the same for ridestore.com, redbull.com, mdpi.com, trick-tips.com). **Every citation below comes from WebSearch snippets or summaries**, not from reading the full pages. Quoted wording is near-verbatim at best. Forum/Reddit material is labelled anecdotal. Material already in notes 01/05/06/07/08 is referred to, not repeated. **[DERIVATION]** = my own arithmetic; **[INFERENCE]** = my reasoning, no source.

Design being checked: charge power (spin size) → S width/length; S starts at charge start and has fixed length; on time = straight centred takeoff on the right edge + full rotation help; late = S cut by the lip, off-line exit; early = S finished before the lip, rider rides straight to the lip and LOSES the carve's rotation help.

## Q1. Do bigger spins use a bigger/longer/deeper set-up carve, or the same line with more wind-up?

### Takeaway
**Contradicted as stated.** No coaching source found says the set-up S gets wider or longer for bigger spins. Every source says the entry path is the same for all spins; what scales with spin size is the upper-body pre-wind, the speed of its release, pop, and (indirectly) speed/airtime from a bigger jump. Coaches explicitly warn against carving harder to get rotation. The only crumb in favour is SA's "set up carves will also add momentum to your rotation", which is qualitative and not tied to spin size.

### Cited Findings
- "Don't carve too hard in your set up, you don't want to rely on the set up for your rotation. You should still be using your upper body to initiate the spin." Same page: "Set up carves will also add momentum to your rotation." — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths For Spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- SA, all backside spins: "The ideal entry path for all Backside spins is to set up with a smooth heel edge carve towards the side of the jump then transfer to your toe edge while riding up the lip." ("all" = one path regardless of rotation). — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths For Spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- Extra rotation comes "by spinning faster out of your shoulders and arms and of course by hitting bigger jumps and having more time in the air" (search summary; no mention of a bigger carve). — [Ridestore, How to rotate on a snowboard](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-rotate-on-a-snowboard/)
- SA places the arm/shoulder wind-up **at the setup point** and says "the faster you release your wind up, the quicker you will spin." — [SA, How To Backside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps); [SA, How To Spin 360's, 540's & 720's](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps)
- Speed for spins: the same as straight airs "or a smidge faster" (search summary). — [SA, How To Spin 360's, 540's & 720's](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps)
- Already in note 01: "A 360, 540 and 720 all start the same way" (Trick-Tips); 540 vs 360 differs only by "a slight bit of extra effort put into your pre-wind" (SA BS 540). Not repeated here.
- Over-carving for the jump size causes over-spinning: a forum recollection of "winding and edging too much for the size of the jump" (search summary; anecdotal). — [Boardworld forum archive](https://boardworld.com.au/forums/viewreply/32528)
- Triple cork (1440) off the toes: "Carve up the take off as if you were going to spin backside", then "throw yourself forward and the other way" at the last moment. Describes the edge/line, not a bigger S. — [Whitelines, Sochi 2014 guide to triple corks](https://whitelines.com/snowboard-culture/snowboard-events/sochi-2014-essential-guide-to-triple-corks.html/9)
- Biomechanics (BS 720→1800, 11 Chinese national-team athletes): most takeoff metrics rise with difficulty; the authors attribute harder tricks to larger moment of inertia at takeoff, more propulsion and joint kinematics that "build rotational energy". The snippets mention no approach-turn variable. — [Jiang et al., Applied Sciences 2025, 15(12):6618](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/12/6618)
- Jyväskylä thesis (BS 360/540/720): joint angular velocities varied between tricks, but subject-dependently; no approach-carve measure in the snippets. — [JYX thesis](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/81576)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] A game mapping "charge → wider/longer S" is not realistic as primary mapping. Realistic: charge → pre-wind magnitude (torque/angular-momentum budget) and release rate; S geometry roughly constant per jump. A *small* growth of S amplitude with charge (e.g. +10–20 %) is defensible only as "a bit more edge/commitment" (SA's "carves add momentum"; forum "winding and edging too much ... over-spinning"), not as a documented rule.
- [INFERENCE] If the game keeps "charge → S size" for readability, a softer version is: S **depth/edge angle of the final takeoff-edge arc** grows slightly with charge, length fixed. That matches "carve up the take off" + "edging" scaling, better than a wider lateral swing.

### Gaps
- No source gives S width/length per spin size (180/360/720/1080). No competition-commentary or pro-interview quote found on "bigger spin = bigger set-up". Applied Sciences 2025 full text unreadable (mdpi.com blocked), so whether it measured approach curvature is unknown.

## Q2. Early set-up: does the rider lose the rotation help, or keep turning on the lip?

### Takeaway
**Coaching describes the opposite failure.** "Setting up too early" is described as the board still turning / turning *too much* on the lip: pre-rotation, scrubbing speed, diagonal exit, drift — i.e. *excess* (badly timed) rotation, not lost rotation. The design's specific case (S complete, base flat, ride straight up the lip) is not described by any coach as "early"; the closest evidence is the general rule that you spin off an **edge**, not a flat base, because the edge gives grip to push off, and that a flat-base takeoff tends to slide. So "losing help" is **partly supported by inference** for a flat-base straight run-in, but it is not how coaches describe an early set-up.

### Cited Findings
- "If you set up too early you will end up not going in a straight direction, and you will have made some of your rotation even before you leave the jump"; an early setup "means you'll end up turning too much in the lip" (search summary). — [SA, How To Spin 360's, 540's & 720's](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps)
- Riders "carve too far and end up already spinning some of the 360 and 'scrubbing' off the lip ... This problem will totally kill your momentum and air stability." — [SA, Top 3 problems FS 360](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/16098668-top-3-problems-when-learning-how-to-frontside-360-on-a-snowboard)
- Pre-rotation = "turning or spinning too early up the face of the jump"; "typically reduces speed", causing knuckle landings and drifting off the landing. — [Maverix, The keys to landing FS 180s (Medium)](https://medium.com/@maverixsnow/the-keys-to-landing-frontside-180-s-on-a-snowboard-1ad6fdcf8ed9)
- Classic 180 problem: "a set up carve doesn't work out and you fly off on a diagonal"; ideally take off "with your board perpendicular to the jump". — [SA, How To Improve Your Frontside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/news/16927500-the-ultimate-guide-to-frontside-180s-on-a-snowboard)
- Edge vs flat base: "You always spin off an edge, rather than a flat base because the edge gives you something to grip and push off the snow with"; "if you take off from a flat base, your board tends to just slide across the snow and can lead to catching an edge on landing" (search summary; page attribution among the SA/Whitelines results unclear). — [SA, Entry Paths For Spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning); [Whitelines, FS 180 with Elias Elhardt](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-frontside-180-with-elias-elhardt.html)
- Contradiction: an SA FS 360 page says "As you ride up the jump, try to keep a flat base with your weight slightly over your heels" — i.e. some coaches want a near-flat (lightly heel-weighted) base up the ramp, not a hard edge. — [SA, How to frontside 360 on park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-360-on-park-jumps)
- Reddit (anecdotal), "Why do I stall out on 360": one commenter says the rider "isn't generating spin off the takeoff" and is "just prewinding and bringing their feet around" in the air; "upon takeoff you must be carving on an edge like you're turning". Others blame hips/shoulders out of sync and counter-rotation; one says only "a tiny bit of residual momentum" kept the rider going. — [r/snowboardingnoobs thread (mirror)](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/snowboardingnoobs/comments/1jzh0c9/why_do_i_stall_out_on_360)
- Wind-up timing: hold the separation "for a split second" or the take-off scrubs; release "as your front foot comes off the lip"; best release "at the top of the transition" (search summary of several pages). — [SA, How to spin frontside from the toe edge](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-spin-frontside-from-the-toe-edge); [Boardworld forum archive](https://boardworld.com.au/forums/viewreply/32528)
- Note 06 already has the forum line that the pre-wind "has to occur early enough to get a lot of tension built but not so soon you pause your motion" (SnowboardingForum, anecdotal) — the only source hinting that an early, held wind-up loses something.
- The search tool reported explicitly that **no source says holding a wind-up too long makes you lose tension**.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Two distinct "early" cases exist and should not be conflated:
  1. **Coaching "early"** = carve started early, rider still on the takeoff edge and still turning when reaching the lip → *pre-rotation*, scrub, diagonal exit in the spin direction, drift (documented; note 08 has the geometry).
  2. **Design "early"** = S fully finished, board flattened and pointed straight before the ramp → no carving edge at the lip. Evidence for "loses help": "spin off an edge, not a flat base"; Reddit "you must be carving on an edge at takeoff". Evidence against a big loss: rotation is said to come mainly from the upper body, which the rider can still release; one SA page even asks for a flat-ish base up the ramp.
- [INFERENCE] Most realistic game model for case 2: keep full wind-up/pop torque, remove only the small carve term (edge grip + path curvature) and perhaps add a small "slide" penalty (lateral slip / less clean pop). For case 1 (still turning at lip): rotation is *started on the ground* (pre-rotation), costs speed and pushes the exit diagonal — closer to the design's "late" bucket than to its "early" bucket in feel.
- [INFERENCE] Which is more common? Coaching texts mention case 1 far more (every SA spin page); case 2 appears only implicitly. A realistic game might model case 1 as the primary "too early" error.

### Gaps
- No coaching quote uses phrases like "the wind-up unwinds", "you lose the pre-rotation" or "rotation dies" for a straight run-in after an early set-up. None found.
- No measured data on rotation lost with a flat-base vs edged takeoff.

## Q3. Late set-up: still turning / wrong edge at the lip; off-line exit

### Takeaway
**Supported qualitatively**, but thinly. Late set-up is described as a rushed edge change and skidding/short takeoff; leaving "on an angle" or while the board is still turning sends the rider off-line. Note 08 already has the geometry and these sources; little new was found.

### Cited Findings
- A carve still turning at takeoff "sends you off in the wrong direction"; knuckle causes include "leaving the lip on an angle" (already in note 06). — [Trick-Tips, How to 360, 540 and 720](https://www.trick-tips.com/snowboarding/how-to-360-540-and-720-frontside-or-backside)
- The setup point (flat between in-run and lip) is where the set-up must finish and the edge transfer happen. — [SA, How To Backside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps)
- Diagonal release → unintended cork. — [SA FS 180](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/news/16927500-the-ultimate-guide-to-frontside-180s-on-a-snowboard); see note 01 for Trick-Tips/Whitelines.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] "S cut by the lip" = rider reaches the lip before finishing the return arc → still on the set-up (wrong) edge or mid edge change → takeoff on the wrong edge / flat and heading off-axis *against* the spin direction (note 08 derivation). Consistent with the design.

### Gaps
- No new source specifically on "too late" beyond notes 06/08.

## Q4. How much rotation does a well-timed carve add; how much is lost if early/missing?

### Takeaway
**No measured value exists in the sources found.** Coaches say "adds momentum" but "don't rely on it". The only number is the project's own scaling in note 05 (path curvature ≈ 4–15 % of the yaw rate of a big spin). The design's ≈5–15 % is consistent with that derivation and with the coaching hierarchy (upper body ≫ carve), but it is not an observed fact.

### Cited Findings
- "Set up carves will also add momentum to your rotation" / "don't rely on the set up for your rotation". — [SA, Entry Paths For Spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- Takeoff sets flight and rotation; harder tricks show larger takeoff moment of inertia, propulsion, rotational energy. No carve share reported in snippets. — [Jiang et al., Applied Sciences 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/12/6618)
- Angular momentum is fixed at takeoff; only redistribution in the air (tuck/extend). — [Scientific American, Olympic big air snowboarders use physics](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/olympic-big-air-snowboarders-use-physics-to-their-advantage/)

### Inferences
- [DERIVATION] Yaw rate from path curvature ω = v/R. At v = 17 m/s (≈61 km/h) and an arc radius R = 30–50 m (note 07): ω ≈ 0.34–0.57 rad/s ≈ 20–33 °/s. Over 2 s of air that is ≈40–65° if it all carried into the spin. Share of target rotation: 180 → 22–36 %; 360 → 11–18 %; 720 → 6–9 %; 1080 → 4–6 %. So the **relative** carve help shrinks as spin size grows — the opposite of "bigger spin needs bigger S". (Assumes the board is still curving at the lip; if the return arc has fully straightened, path-curvature contribution is ≈0.)
- [INFERENCE] Since a coached takeoff is "straight at the lip", the carve's help is not mainly path curvature but (a) a grippy edge platform to push the release against and (b) the place where the wind-up is loaded (SA: wind-up "at the setup point"). A game could model help as a multiplier on release torque (e.g. ×1.05–1.15 on a clean edge) rather than as extra yaw rate from the S.
- [INFERENCE] Plausible game penalty for design-"early" (flat, straight): lose that 5–15 % edge bonus, maybe a little more on 180 (where carve share is largest). For coaching-"early" (still turning): gain unwanted pre-rotation on the ground + speed scrub + diagonal exit.

### Gaps
- No biomechanics paper isolates the approach-turn contribution to takeoff angular momentum (note 05 found the same).

## Q5. Quantitative: set-up length/time and lateral size per spin size

### Takeaway
**None found.** Sources give only landmarks (start "a ways back", carve "towards the side of the jump", finish at the setup point). Geometry estimates are in notes 07/07a/08 and do not vary with spin size in any source.

### Cited Findings
- "Start a ways back and line up a toe edge carve to the left of the jump, transitioning to heel edge as you approach the jump. You want your set up carve to bring you to the lip of the jump with your board in a straight direction." — [SA, Entry Paths For Spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/entry-paths-for-spinning)
- Rhythm Japan (Bode): airtime first, "confident approach speed, a tall pop, and a smooth rotation" for a first 360; no set-up dimensions (search summary). — [Rhythm Japan, From 0 to 360°](https://rhythmjapan.com/our-stories/tips-from-the-team-360)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] For the game: keep S length fixed (as designed) and tie it to the jump (in-run/setup-point distance), not to charge. If S must vary with charge, vary it little and document it as a gameplay choice, not research-backed.
