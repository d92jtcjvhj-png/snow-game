> Conventions: REGULAR rider = left foot forward. Heel edge = edge on the rider's back side. For a regular rider riding forward, a heelside turn goes to the rider's left (looking downhill), and a toeside turn goes to the right. Frontside (FS) spin = chest/front faces downhill after the first 90°; for a regular rider forward this is counter-clockwise viewed from above. Backside (BS) = back faces downhill first, clockwise from above for a regular rider ([Wikipedia: Frontside and backside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontside_and_backside)).

## 1. Which edge for FS and BS spins (regular rider), and why

### Takeaway
Strong consensus: **FS spins take off from the heel edge, BS spins from the toe edge** (the "natural" setups). FS off the toes is a recognised and fairly common variant ("hardway"/"off the toes"), especially on 180s and steep takeoffs. BS off the heels is rare. The default for big FS spins on big jumps is heel edge. A pure flat-base takeoff is discouraged for spins because the board slips. A *slight* edge is enough.

### Cited Findings
- "Frontside rotations are naturally set from the heel edge, whilst backside rotations are set from the toe edge. So a frontside spin off the toes, or backside rotation off the heels is typically called a 'hardway' takeoff." Search-result summary of coaching/forum pages. The exact originating page among the results is uncertain. Candidate pages: [Snowboardingforum: front 3s off toes AND heels](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/anyone-here-spin-front-3s-off-their-toes-and-heels-whats-the-difference.282598/), [Snowboardingforum: Hucking frontside off the toes](http://www.snowboardingforum.com/tips-tricks-snowboard-coaching/40753-hucking-frontside-off-toes.html), [Trick-Tips 360/540/720](https://www.trick-tips.com/snowboarding/how-to-360-540-and-720-frontside-or-backside).
- "For a Frontside 180 you can take off with either your heel edge or your toe edge. This tutorial focuses on using your heel edge which is the more common approach for Frontside spinning." — [Snowboard Addiction: How to Frontside 180 on park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-180-on-park-jumps)
- Spinning FS from the heel edge is described as "much easier and the best place to start from." — [Snowboard Addiction: How to spin 360s, 540s, 720s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps)

- Glossary-type summary: "it's easiest to leave on your heel edge from a frontside or cab spin, and off your toes for backside or switch backside." Search snippet. The source page is uncertain, likely an SA or REI glossary page in the result set ([REI Snowboarding Glossary](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/snowboarding-glossary.html)).

- "There are no hard and fast rules when you're spinning frontside for which edge you spin off. Some people prefer their toes; others prefer their heels." — Whitelines snippet ([How to Frontside 180 with Elias Elhardt](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-frontside-180-with-elias-elhardt.html))
- Terrain-dependent rule: "If the take off is quite flat then it will be better taking off from your heels; if it's steep, it's your toes." — coaching snippet in the FS-off-toes result set (likely [Whitelines FS 360 Ben Kilner](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-fs-360-ben-kilner.html); attribution uncertain).
- Whitelines (Ben Kilner FS 360) describes a **toe-edge FS 360**: "Keep the board in a straight line and pre-wind your shoulders ... then gently dig your toe edge into the kicker and pop hard into the spin, unleashing your arms." — [Whitelines FS 360 Indy, Ben Kilner](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-fs-360-ben-kilner.html). This **contradicts** SA's heel-edge default for FS 360.
- Forum riders: FS off the toes "feels like it has so much less amplitude"; "as spins get bigger and jumps get larger, it's much more common to use a heel edge to spin frontside." — [Snowboardingforum thread](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/anyone-here-spin-front-3s-off-their-toes-and-heels-whats-the-difference.282598/) (anecdotal)
- Biomechanics of the heel-edge FS takeoff: the heel edge "already provides some spin momentum and may be enough to get you all the way around". The rider shifts "weight onto your heels as you approach the takeoff, then spring upwards off the tail, throwing the spin with your arms." — SA/Whitelines snippets ([SA FS 180](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-180-on-park-jumps); [Whitelines Elhardt FS 180](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-frontside-180-with-elias-elhardt.html))
- Whitelines describes the FS rotation as turning "your front toward the landing as you enter the air, like a heelside turn." — [Whitelines FS 180 Elhardt](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-frontside-180-with-elias-elhardt.html)
- FS off the toes needs **upper-body momentum** (the edge does not help the rotation), and counter-rotation works for 180s but limits bigger spins. — [SA: Advanced Frontside 180](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-advanced-frontside-180-on-a-snowboard)
- BS toe-edge takeoff: create edge angle "by moving hips and knees forward rather than leaning over the edge" and stay upright, so you stay "flat and balanced while spinning". — [SA: How to Backside 360 on park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps). Whitelines adds "a bit of pressure on your toes as you leave the lip ... don't lean back or you will go into a cork." — [Whitelines BS 360 tail](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/how-to-backside-360-tail.html)
- Edge vs flat base: "The grip you get from taking off on an edge will help you initiate the spin. However, if you take off from a flat base, your board tends to just slide across the snow." — SA snippet ([Entry paths](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/entry-paths-for-spinning)). Also: "If you try to jump a Frontside 180 from a flat base, you'll feel your board slipping out. Instead, use your edges to grip the snow." — [SA: Your first frontside 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/your-first-frontside-180s-on-a-snowboard)
- Riders take off "from an edge, even a slight one, which gives them something to push against." — [Snowboarding Days: rotation and counter-rotation](https://www.snowboardingdays.com/snowboarding-freestyle-basics-the-concepts-of-rotation-and-counter-rotation/)
- Contrasting beginner advice: Burton says keep the base flat and knees bent on approach and ollie as the nose leaves the lip (straight airs). — [Burton: Snowboard tricks to learn](https://www.burton.com/blogs/the-burton-blog/snowboard-tricks-to-learn). Whitelines describes the 180 approach "on a flat base, with your weight centred". — [Whitelines Elhardt FS 180](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-frontside-180-with-elias-elhardt.html). Reconciliation: the approach can be flat, but the takeoff instant is on a slight edge.
- Ridestore: the FS 180 takes off on the heel edge and aims to land on the toe edge. — [Ridestore: first frontside 180](https://ridestore.com/mag/how-to-do-your-first-frontside-180-on-a-snowboard)

### Inferences
- Mechanism (inference consistent with sources): on the heel edge a regular rider is already carving left (CCW from above), the same direction as FS rotation. The edge's turning moment plus the shoulder unwind add up. The toe edge carves right (CW), matching BS. So the "natural" edge is the one whose carve already rotates the board in the spin direction. The "hardway" edge works against the carve, and all the rotation must come from the upper body plus pop.
- Game default: FS → heel edge, BS → toe edge. A per-rider or per-kicker "hardway" toggle can be optional. Use the toe edge for FS only on small spins (180/360) or steep lips, if at all.

- No AASI/PSIA, BASI, CASI or FIS coaching document with explicit edge rules was found. Only an unverified forum claim about AASI terminology surfaced.
- No primary source on which edge specific pros use for 1080+ in big air was found.

## 2. Shape of the approach (set-up turn, distance, edge hold, carve vs skid, line)

### Takeaway
Consensus: **one set-up turn on the opposite edge, then a smooth transition to the takeoff edge, held up the ramp so the board leaves the lip travelling straight** (not across the jump, not still turning). The takeoff edge is a clean, low-angle carve, not a skid. Sources say "one smooth turn" and "carving roughly in line with the side of the jump". No source gives a numeric distance.

### Cited Findings
- FS (heel takeoff): "Do one smooth toe edge turn carving roughly in line with the side of the jump. Then, transition to your heel edge for a smooth carve up and off the lip." — [SA: FS 180 on park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-180-on-park-jumps)
- FS 540: "Start your approach with a toe edge set up carve, then transition to your heel edge to build momentum for your frontside spin." — [SA: How to Frontside 540](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-540)
- Trick-Tips, generic FS: "make one setup turn on your toe edge, then carve up the lip on your heel edge." The setup turn keeps the board "travelling straight" as it leaves the lip, so you don't fly off in the direction of the turn. BS mirrors this: heel setup turn, then toe edge up the lip. — [Trick-Tips](https://www.trick-tips.com/snowboarding/how-to-360-540-and-720-frontside-or-backside)
- Another snippet: a "toeside set up turn and transfer on to a heelside platform on your way up the jump." — [SA: 360s/540s/720s on large jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps)
- **Conflicting SA description:** one SA snippet says "The ideal entry path for all Frontside spins is to set up with a heel turn, then a smooth toe edge carve towards the side of the jump." That is a heel → toe → heel sequence: the toe carve is the *set-up* toward the side of the jump, and the final heel edge goes up the lip. — [SA: Entry paths for spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/entry-paths-for-spinning). Reading it alongside the FS 180 page, it is the same pattern with an extra earlier heel turn to get wide. It is not a real contradiction.
- BS 180/360: "The optimal entry path is going wide and coming in on the heel edge, as you reach the jump, shift to the toe edge of your snowboard. You should leave the jump off of your toe-edge going in a straight direction off the jump." — [SA: Improve your BS 180s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/17257876-improve-your-backside-180s). SA's BS 360 path is the same as BS 180: heel carve toward the jump, then a transfer to the toe edge while riding up the lip. — [SA: BS 360 park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps)
- FS off the toes (hardway): "the entry path changes to a heel edge setup carve transitioning to a toe edge platform on the lip." — [SA: Advanced FS 180](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-advanced-frontside-180-on-a-snowboard)
- Pushdalimit FS 360: "toeside carve coming in diagonally, then switch to the heel edge so you take off on your heels but go completely straight off the takeoff." — [Pushdalimit: FS 360](https://pushdalimit.com/tutorial/how-to-frontside-360-on-a-snowboard/)
- Straight-line variant: Whitelines (Kilner) says travel in a straight line right up to the take-off instead of carving hard on the heel edge, then "gently dig" the edge. — [Whitelines FS 360](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-fs-360-ben-kilner.html)
- Don't over-carve: "don't carve too hard in your set up, you don't want to rely on the set up for your rotation. You should still be using your upper body to initiate the spin." Also: "bring you to the lip of the jump with your board in a straight direction". Setting up too early means "You'll end up turning too much in the lip." — snippets from the SA spin pages / [Boardworld forum archive](https://boardworld.com.au/forums/viewreply/32528)
- Carve, not skid: "If we don't edge the board as we head up to jump and take off, we will slide and skid the board as we leave the lip". This costs speed and distance and often leaves the rider short on the knuckle. — [Key Aspects Coaching: 360 setup / take off & stability](https://www.keyaspectscoaching.com/freestyle-tips/tips-mondays-360-setup-take-off-stability/) (snippet)
- Number of turns and speed management (forum): "I like doing two [setup carves] beforehand, too many throws me off." Check speed early, not at the base of the ramp, so balance is regained "before the board enters the ramp zone." — [Snowboardingforum: scrubbing speed before jumps](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/scrubbing-speed-before-jumps.35209/) and related threads (anecdotal)
- Body timing: lowest crouch "just as you come to the transition leading up to the jump", extending up the face to be fully extended at the lip. — [Snowboardingforum: Proper jump procedure](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/proper-jump-procedure.136777/) (anecdotal). SA: pop by flexing and extending the legs off the jump. — [SA FS 180](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-180-on-park-jumps)

### Inferences (implementation-oriented, NOT sourced numbers)
- Recommended model: (a) the set-up turn on the opposite edge ends around the start of the transition (the bottom of the ramp). (b) The edge change to the takeoff edge happens at or just before the transition. (c) The rider holds a *low* edge angle on the takeoff edge up the whole ramp face, with heading ≈ ramp axis (yaw rate ≈ 0 at the lip). (d) Pop and release happen at the lip.
- "Carving roughly in line with the side of the jump" suggests the set-up turn's lateral offset is about half the kicker width. The rider drifts to one side, then arcs back so the takeoff-edge carve ends aligned with the ramp centreline. This is an inference.
- The takeoff carve is subtle. Several sources warn against hard carving at the lip because the rider flies off in the turn's direction. Model edge angle up the ramp as small (well below set-up turn angle). No source quantifies degrees.

### Gaps
- No source gives a distance in metres, a turn radius, or edge-angle numbers. No source ties the start of the set-up turn to the knuckle or transition in measurable terms.
- No source quantifies how much lateral offset or how much residual angle across the kicker is acceptable.

## 3. How the approach changes with rotation (180 → 360 → 540/720 → 1080+)

### Takeaway
The entry path is essentially the **same** for 360, 540 and 720. The main extra input for bigger rotation is **more pre-wind / faster release and a stronger pop**, not a radically harder carve. Speed stays about the same as a straight air of the same jump, maybe a touch more. A 180 needs little or no pre-wind.

### Cited Findings
- "A 360, 540 and 720 all start the same way." — [Trick-Tips](https://www.trick-tips.com/snowboarding/how-to-360-540-and-720-frontside-or-backside)
- "The bigger you want to spin, the more wind up is needed." For the BS 540: "The only difference needed to spin a 540 compared to a 360 is a slight bit of extra effort put into your pre-wind. This will help you create more torque when popping off the lip." — [SA: How to Backside 540](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-540); [Ski Addiction 540/720](https://skiaddiction.com/blogs/tutorials/540s-720s)
- Speed: "use the same speed as your straight airs, or a tiny bit faster." — [SA large-jump spins](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps)
- Carving into the takeoff can add rotation on bigger spins, "but you need to do a set up carve so that you are still going straight off the jump." — [SA large-jump spins](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps)
- "A strong pop will allow you to spin faster and smoother." — [SA BS 540](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-540)
- BS 360 vs BS 180: release the rotation faster than for the 180. Combine a good pop off the toe edge with a faster release of the wind-up. — [SA: BS 360 park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps)
- "The faster you release the quicker you will spin"; "the more tension you can create, the faster you will spin." — snippets from the SA spin pages ([Entry paths](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/entry-paths-for-spinning))
- 180: little or no pre-rotation needed. — [Key Aspects Coaching: FS 180](http://www.keyaspectscoaching.com/freestyle-tips/how-to-frontside-180-on-a-snowboard-toe-edge/). Mountainwatch: the FS 360 "doesn't need too much pre-wind." — [Mountainwatch FS 360](https://www.mountainwatch.com/?p=9107)
- BS 540: "throw your shoulders 270 off the jump and float the last 270." — [Ed Shreds: BS 540](https://www.edshreds.com/how-to-backside-540-on-a-snowboard/). Whitelines: drive the shoulder toward the back foot and extend the legs to pop. — [Whitelines BS 540 Claire Frost](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/backside-5s-claire-frost.html)
- Ridestore: over-rotating → reduce arm wind-up; under-rotating → more pop from the back foot. — [Ridestore first FS 180](https://ridestore.com/mag/how-to-do-your-first-frontside-180-on-a-snowboard)

### Inferences
- For a game: keep the path geometry constant across spin sizes. Scale pre-wind magnitude (shoulder counter-rotation before the lip) and release speed with target rotation. Optionally add a slight increase in takeoff-edge angle or carve contribution for 720+ (supported only qualitatively by "carving can add rotation"). Speed is set by the jump size, not by the spin.

### Gaps
- No source on 900/1080+ approach specifics (e.g. whether pros deepen the set-up turn, change edge angle, or approach faster). No competition-commentary source was found.

## 4. Switch and cab spins

### Takeaway
Same edge logic applied to the *riding direction*: switch FS (cab) takes off from the **heel edge** with a toe set-up turn, and switch BS from the **toe edge** with a heel set-up turn. Physically, the edges swap relative to the world turning direction because the rider faces the same way while the board travels tail-first.

### Cited Findings
- Cab = switch FS spin; a cab 360 is a switch FS 360. — [Wikipedia: Caballerial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caballerial); [SA: How to Caballerial](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-do-caballerial-snowboard-trick)
- SA: initiate a switch front 3 "with your heel edge and a bit of a wind up of your upper body." — [SA: Caballerial](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-do-caballerial-snowboard-trick)
- Easiest edges: heel for FS or cab, toes for BS or switch BS. — glossary snippet (see section 1; source page uncertain)
- Switch BS 540: "a heel to toe approach, coming in switch on our heels and riding up the jump on our toes." Riders often panic, pre-spin or bend at the waist on the blind takeoff. The trick is framed as a switch BS 180 into an FS 360 with the same takeoff and axis. — [Ed Shreds: Switch BS 540](https://www.edshreds.com/how-to-switch-backside-540-on-a-snowboard/)

### Inferences
- Implementation: compute FS/BS relative to the rider's current direction of travel, then apply "FS → heel takeoff with toe set-up; BS → toe takeoff with heel set-up". For a regular rider riding switch, the right foot leads, so the heel edge now carves to the right in world terms. The world direction of the set-up turns mirrors the forward case.

### Gaps
- No source on approach differences for switch (e.g. wider set-up, less speed). Only the general note that switch takeoffs are less comfortable.

## 5. Common errors related to the approach edge

### Takeaway
Main errors: skidding or slipping the takeoff (especially FS off the heels), pre-rotating or releasing too early (the board turns on the ramp), setting up too early and over-turning at the lip, a diagonal or leaning release that causes a cork or off-axis spin, and leaning too far forward on BS toe takeoffs.

### Cited Findings
- Slipping or skidding on the lip "is one of the biggest problems with 360s, especially frontside off the heel edge". It is caused by not edging up the ramp, often by the back foot losing grip. It costs speed and distance and leaves the rider short on the knuckle. — [Key Aspects Coaching: 360 setup](https://www.keyaspectscoaching.com/freestyle-tips/tips-mondays-360-setup-take-off-stability/) (snippet)
- Early pre-wind leads to an early release, a pop off the tail and an off-axis spin. Hip pre-rotation triggers board rotation (a skid). Keep pre-rotation in the shoulders and arms and hold the spin until further up the jump, with the board straight at the lip. — [Snowboardingforum: preventing early rotation for FS spin](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/how-to-prevent-early-rotation-for-a-fs-spin.17194/) and [clean non-skidded FS spins thread](https://www.snowboardingforum.com/threads/can-you-do-clean-non-skidded-frontside-spins-off-your-heals.273412/) (anecdotal)
- Setting up too early causes "turning too much in the lip". — SA / [Boardworld](https://boardworld.com.au/forums/viewreply/32528) snippet
- A diagonal release produces a corked spin. — [Trick-Tips](https://www.trick-tips.com/snowboarding/how-to-360-540-and-720-frontside-or-backside). Leaning back on a BS toe takeoff sends the rider into a cork. — [Whitelines BS 360](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/how-to-backside-360-tail.html)
- BS 360: leaning too far forward on approach (dragging a hand, bending over the toe edge) is a frequent cause of missed spins. Edge with hips and knees, not by bending. — [SA BS 360](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps)
- Landing-side edge issues (not takeoff): on a FS 360, land on the toe edge to avoid washing out on the heels, or stay committed so you don't hook the heel edge at 270. — [Pushdalimit FS 360](https://pushdalimit.com/tutorial/how-to-frontside-360-on-a-snowboard/). On a BS 540, a heel-edge catch is painful. — [Ed Shreds BS 540](https://www.edshreds.com/how-to-backside-540-on-a-snowboard/)

### Inferences
- Game-side guardrails: keep yaw rate near zero on the ramp (straight at the lip). Keep lateral slip low on the takeoff edge, or treat skid as a penalty to speed or pop. Don't let pre-wind turn the board. Tilt the spin axis (cork) if the release has a lateral lean.

### Gaps
- No source specifically describes catching the downhill or wrong edge on the lip at takeoff. Edge-catch sources concern landings and turns.
