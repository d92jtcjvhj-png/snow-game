# Approach-line geometry into a kicker before a spin (set-up turn), seen from above

Source access note: every page fetch was blocked by the session network (snowboardaddiction.com, whitelines.com, ridestore.com, instructables.com and mdpi.com all failed DNS: "outside the session's allowed network domains"). Everything cited below comes from **search-engine snippets** of those pages, not from reading the full pages. Quotes are as the snippets gave them. No diagram images could be viewed. All numbers in metres/seconds are **ESTIMATES** (marked as such) worked out from the qualitative descriptions plus basic circular-arc kinematics. I found no source that gives measured lateral offsets or distances.

Conventions: regular rider (left foot forward). Seen from above, going downhill, a regular rider's toes point to the rider's right (skier's right). A **toe-side carve curves the path to skier's right** and a **heel-side carve curves it to skier's left**. Frontside rotation for a regular rider is counter-clockwise seen from above (the same sense as a heel-side turn). Backside is clockwise (the same sense as a toe-side turn).

## 1. Typical approach line: shape, number of arcs, edge sequence (FS heel takeoff / BS toe takeoff)

### Takeoff
The main coaching source (Snowboard Addiction) describes a **2–3 arc S-line**. A first turn is optional. Then comes **one smooth set-up carve on the edge opposite the takeoff edge**, angled "towards the side of the jump". The edge change to the takeoff edge happens late, on the flat "set-up point" or on the transition, so that the board leaves the lip pointing straight. Whitelines describes backside as a "gentle 'S' curve". So the rider does **not** ride straight down the middle: they cross the kicker axis during the set-up arc and come back onto the axis for the takeoff. Whitelines also says (for the frontside 540) that the rider should go up the kicker "in almost a straight line, NOT carving up it". The two sources agree on the final straight takeoff and differ on how much curve the approach has.

### Cited Findings
- **Frontside (heel takeoff), heel–toe–heel:** "The ideal entry path for all Frontside spins is to set up with a heel turn, then a smooth toe edge carve towards the side of the jump. When you're at the set up point, get on another heel edge. This heel, toe, heel line of entry allows you to be on your heel edge as you leave the lip of the jump, but still fly in a straight direction while in the air." — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths For Spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/entry-paths-for-spinning) (search snippet)
- The same heel–toe–heel line is repeated for frontside 360s on park jumps — [Snowboard Addiction, How To Frontside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-360-on-park-jumps) (snippet)
- The frontside 540 uses the same pattern: "Start your approach with a toe edge set up carve, then transition to your heel edge to build momentum for your frontside spin." — [Snowboard Addiction, How To Frontside 540](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/how-to-frontside-540) (snippet)
- Ridestore summarises frontside as "one smooth toe edge turn just before the jump", which is the same toe set-up arc seen without the first heel turn — [Ridestore, How to rotate on a snowboard](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-rotate-on-a-snowboard/) (snippet, relayed by search summary)
- **Backside (toe takeoff), heel set-up then toe:** "On your approach, do one smooth heel edge turn, carving roughly in line with the side of the jump. Next, transition to your toe edge to carve up and off the lip." — [Snowboard Addiction, How To Backside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps) (snippet). The same site says this is "the same entry path as a Backside 180" and applies to all backside spins — [Snowboard Addiction, How to Backside 180 on park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-180-on-park-jumps) (snippet)
- Whitelines (backside 360): the approach goes from toe edge to heel edge on the in-run, then flattens toward the toes at the transition, so the rider is on the toe edge at the jump. "This whole approach should resemble a gentle 'S' curve." — [Whitelines, Slo-mo trick tips backside 360](https://whitelines.com/trick-tips/kicker-trick-tips/slo-mo-trick-tips-backside-360.html) (snippet)
- Variant (Instructables, amateur): first turn on the toe edge, then the heel edge "toward the jump's center", and "it's important to be on your toe edge when you hit the lip". The first edge is reversed compared with Snowboard Addiction, but the shape is still an S ending on the toe edge — [Instructables, How to Backside 360 on a Snowboard](https://www.instructables.com/How-to-Backside-360-on-a-Snowboard/) (snippet, low authority)
- Frontside off the **toes** (an alternative style) flips the pattern: "When spinning Frontside off the toes the entry path changes to a heel edge setup carve transitioning to a toe edge platform on the lip." — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths For Spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/entry-paths-for-spinning) (snippet). Whitelines/Ben Kilner also mention the "old-school" frontside 360 set up like a backside 360 and taken off the toe edge — [Whitelines, FS 360 Ben Kilner](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/snowboard-fs-360-ben-kilner.html) (snippet)
- The purpose of the set-up carve is to point the board the opposite way to the spin, and to time the carves so the rider faces straight at the lip — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths For Spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/entry-paths-for-spinning); [Snowboard Addiction, Improve your FS 360s](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/spins/16098668-top-3-problems-when-learning-how-to-frontside-360-on-a-snowboard) (snippets)
- Whitelines, frontside 540: "Most people feel happiest throwing a frontside 540 off their heels, but if the jump requires it … get that toe edge dug in. Either way you should still be heading up the kicker in almost a straight line, NOT carving up it." — [Whitelines, How to frontside 540 indy](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-frontside-540-indy.html) (snippet)
- Red Bull's basic jump guide (straight airs) advises straight-lining the jump at moderate speed with no speed checks on the approach — [Red Bull, Snowboard jumps trick tips](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/snowboard-jumps-trick-tips) (snippet)

### Inferences
- **Geometric rule (inferred from the edge sequences above):** the last set-up arc is on the edge *opposite* the takeoff edge, so it curves *against* the spin direction. The takeoff edge, set on the flat or the transition, curves *with* the spin direction. In plan view this makes an S whose final, small bend matches the direction of rotation.
- **Regular, frontside (heel takeoff), plan view:** (optional) heel turn → path heads to skier's left. Toe set-up carve → the path swings through the fall line and heads diagonally to skier's **right**, "towards the side of the jump". At the set-up point, heel edge → the path curves back **left** until it is parallel with the kicker axis at the lip. The rider therefore crosses the axis during the toe arc and reaches the lip coming from the left half, aligned or very slightly converging. (This reading follows from the stated edges plus the toe-right / heel-left convention. No source draws it.)
- **Regular, backside (toe takeoff), plan view:** heel set-up carve curving to skier's **left**, "roughly in line with the side of the jump". Then a toe edge on the lip, curving right, ending aligned with the axis. Mirror image of frontside.
- **Goofy riders:** mirror everything left↔right.
- **Number of arcs for the game:** 2 arcs is the minimum (set-up arc + takeoff-edge arc). 3 arcs if the rider starts from a traverse or from the far side of the in-run (heel–toe–heel for FS). The Whitelines "almost straight" advice means the arcs should be shallow (small heading change), not hairpin turns.

### Gaps
- There is no published plan-view diagram with dimensions. The Snowboard Addiction "Entry Paths" article almost certainly has a video or diagram, but the host was blocked, so it could not be checked.
- "Carving roughly in line with the side of the jump" can be read two ways. (a) The set-up arc's widest point lines up with the kicker's side edge. (b) The board's heading points at the side of the takeoff. The snippets do not settle which.

## 2. Lateral offset and where the set-up turn starts and ends; scaling with jump size and speed

### Takeoff
**No source gives metres or seconds.** The only spatial anchors are qualitative. The set-up arc heads "towards / in line with the side of the jump", which puts the widest lateral excursion at roughly **half the kicker width** from the axis (estimate). The set-up arc ends and the edge change happens at the **"set-up point"**: the flat stretch between the bottom of the in-run and the start of the lip transition. Its position "varies depending on how big the jump is". Setting up too early makes the board turn too much on the lip.

### Cited Findings
- The set-up point is the flat ground between the downhill run-in and the uphill lip, where the rider finishes the set-up turn and transfers to the takeoff platform. Its location varies with jump size — [Snowboard Addiction, How to spin FS 180s/360s/540s/720s on large jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps) (snippet)
- "Setting up too early means you'll end up turning too much in the lip." — same source (snippet, also relayed via [Red Bull search summary](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/snowboard-jumps-trick-tips))
- Backside: the switch to the toe edge should happen "as you are rolling up the lip of the jump, so your board leaves in a straight direction" — [Snowboard Addiction, How To Backside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps) (snippet)
- Ridestore places the frontside toe set-up "just before the jump" — [Ridestore, How to rotate on a snowboard](https://www.ridestore.com/mag/how-to-rotate-on-a-snowboard/) (snippet relay)
- Kicker sizes vary widely ("five feet to 100 feet & above"), with no standard. No source found gives kicker width — [Snowboarding Days, Snowboard jump sizes](https://www.snowboardingdays.com/?p=5486) (snippet)
- One aggregator says big-air in-runs are ~30–40 m long with speeds up to 70 km/h. **Unreliable:** the same snippet gives a "kicker angled at 70–80 degrees", which is physically wrong for a big-air takeoff, so treat the whole entry with suspicion — [Grokipedia, Big air](https://grokipedia.com/page/Big_air)

### Inferences (ESTIMATES, not sourced)
Arc kinematics: a turn of radius R through a heading change Δθ moves the rider sideways by R(1−cos Δθ) and forward by R·sin Δθ. Lateral acceleration is v²/R.

| Case | Speed | Plausible set-up arc R | Heading change per arc | Lateral per arc | Length per arc | Lateral g |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small park (≈5 m gap) | 30–40 km/h (≈8–11 m/s) | 15–20 m | 20–25° | 0.9–1.9 m | 5–8.5 m | 0.4–0.8 g |
| Medium (≈10–15 m) | ≈50 km/h (14 m/s) | 20–33 m | 12–20° | 0.7–1.2 m | ≈7 m | 0.6–1.0 g |
| Big air (20 m+) | 55–75 km/h (15–21 m/s) | 33–50 m | ≈10–12° | 0.6–0.8 m | 7–9 m | 0.7–1.2 g at R=33–50 m |

Computed values: R=20 m with Δθ=20° gives 1.21 m sideways over 6.84 m. R=33 m with Δθ=12° gives 0.72 m over 6.86 m. At 18 m/s, R=33 m needs 1.0 g and R=50 m needs 0.66 g. At 11 m/s, R=20 m needs 0.62 g.

- **Estimate, peak lateral offset of the S from the kicker axis:** about 1–2.5 m on small park jumps and 1–2 m on big air. That is about **0.3–0.5 × kicker width**, consistent with "towards the side of the jump", if takeoffs are about 4–6 m wide (park) and 6–10 m wide (big air). Takeoff widths are an unsourced guess; see Gaps.
- **Estimate, timing:** the set-up arc plus the edge change lasts about **1–1.5 s** and covers about **10–20 m** (small park) to **15–25 m** (big air) before the lip. It ends on the flat set-up point, roughly 0–5 m before the start of the transition. The takeoff-edge phase lasts from the set-up point to the lip: about the transition length, 2–5 m on park jumps and 5–10 m on big air, so about 0.3–0.6 s.
- **Scaling:** at higher speed the heading change shrinks and the radius grows (to keep lateral g ≈ 1 g). So the S gets **longer and flatter**, with a similar or smaller *absolute* lateral offset and a much smaller offset *relative* to kicker width. This matches Whitelines' "almost a straight line" for bigger spins and Red Bull's straight-lining advice. Sources describe the set-up point moving with jump size but give no rule.

### Gaps
- There are no measured lateral offsets, no distances from the kicker base, and no drone or overhead analysis. I found no published kicker-width standards in metres (no FIS/park-builder spec found).
- I could not verify how much bigger the set-up is for 1080+ in big air.

## 3. On the ramp: slight carve up the transition or straight? Where on the lip relative to centre?

### Takeoff
Sources agree on the outcome: **the board leaves the lip pointing straight** (aligned with the kicker axis). They differ on whether the path still curves on the ramp. Snowboard Addiction has the rider "carve up and off the lip" on the takeoff edge (backside) and be "on your heel edge as you leave the lip" (frontside). That edge implies a gentle residual curve in the spin direction. Whitelines says to go up "in almost a straight line, NOT carving up it". The best reconciliation is a **very slight edge-curve on the ramp**, with the edge set late so the heading change on the ramp is small. A takeoff to the side of centre is not described. Riders aim to reach the lip aligned with the axis.

### Cited Findings
- Backside: "transition to your toe edge to carve up and off the lip" — [Snowboard Addiction, How To Backside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps) (snippet)
- Frontside: be "on your heel edge as you leave the lip of the jump, but still fly in a straight direction" — [Snowboard Addiction, Entry Paths For Spinning](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/entry-paths-for-spinning) (snippet)
- "You want your set up carve to bring you to the lip of the jump with your board in a straight direction." — [Snowboard Addiction, How To Frontside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-360-on-park-jumps) (snippet)
- The edge angle at takeoff should come from pushing hips/knees forward, not leaning over the edge, to stay upright and flat — [Snowboard Addiction, BS 360 park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-360-on-park-jumps) (snippet)
- "the toe edge takeoff already provides some spin momentum and can be enough to get you all the way around" (backside) — [Snowboard Addiction, How to backside 180 on park jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-backside-180-on-park-jumps) (snippet)
- "Almost a straight line, NOT carving up it" — [Whitelines, How to frontside 540 indy](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/trick-tips/kicker/how-to-frontside-540-indy.html) (snippet)
- Spinning or turning too early up the face reduces speed and causes knuckle landings and drifting off the landing. Do not carve so hard in the set-up that the rider relies on it for rotation — [Snowboard Addiction, large jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/jumping/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps) (snippet)
- Cliff-drop frontside 360: slightly on the heel edge at the lip, not carving much — [Whitelines, FS 360 off a cliff with Gigi Rüf](https://whitelines.com/snowboarding-advice/backcountry/how-to-frontside-360-off-a-cliff-with-gigi-ruf.html) (snippet)
- Research context: torso "pre-swing" rotation happens in the initial takeoff phase and drives the lower-limb rotation and the board's takeoff angle (halfpipe IMU study) — [PMC, Take-off and landing techniques in snowboard halfpipe](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13618448). Upper-trunk twist and shoulder abduction at takeoff rise with trick difficulty in big air (backside 720–1800, Chinese national team, small sample) — [Applied Sciences 2025, 15(12):6618](https://doi.org/10.3390/app15126618) (snippet)

### Inferences (ESTIMATES)
- Model the ramp as a residual arc on the takeoff edge with a large radius (estimate R ≈ 25–60 m) and a total heading change of only a few degrees (≈2–5°) between the start of the transition and the lip. Heading at the lip ≈ the kicker axis, within a few degrees. Lateral drift on the ramp is ≤ 0.2–0.3 m. Takeoff point is about the centre of the lip, within ≈ ±0.5 m (estimate).
- The S should be timed so the heading crosses zero (parallel to the axis) at about the lip, with lateral position ≈ 0. Arriving aligned *and* centred is the goal. An early edge change leaves the board still turning on the lip, which coaches call a fault.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the residual curvature on the ramp or the takeoff position across the lip. No contest-footage or overhead analysis was found.

## 4. Pure carve or partly skidded at big-air speeds (55–75 km/h)? How do riders get a pronounced set-up at speed?

### Takeoff
Coaching sources call for a **carved** set-up, specifically to *avoid* speed checks (skids) that bleed speed and cause knuckle landings. No source addresses skid fraction at 55–75 km/h directly. Kinematics suggest that at big-air speed the set-up has to be a long, shallow, large-radius arc (to stay near ≤ 1 g). The "pronounced" rotational wind-up then comes mostly from the upper body: pre-wound shoulders and trunk twist, increasing with difficulty. A sharper path is not needed.

### Cited Findings
- "Carving your turns on the approach, rather than counter rotating, allows you to keep almost all of your speed." — [Snowboard Addiction, How To Frontside 360 On Park Jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/how-to-frontside-360-on-park-jumps) (snippet)
- The set-up carve replaces last-minute speed checks. Use the same speed as a straight air, or slightly more, and avoid speed checks just before the jump, which cause knuckle landings — [Snowboard Addiction, large jumps](https://snowboardaddiction.com/blogs/training-education/10481297-how-to-spin-frontside-180s-360s-540s-720s-on-large-jumps) (snippet)
- Skid vs carve: in a skid the board points across the slope while momentum carries the rider downhill. More carved turns keep the board closer to the fall line — [The Manual, snowboarding tips to change edges](https://themanual.com/outdoors/snowboarding-tips-to-change-edges) (snippet)
- Upper-trunk twist and shoulder abduction at takeoff rose with difficulty (reported gains of 121.1% and 98.6%), and higher difficulty brings higher takeoff velocity and angular momentum — [Applied Sciences 2025, Biomechanical factors in snowboard big air takeoff](https://doi.org/10.3390/app15126618) (snippet. The full text was not readable because mdpi.com was blocked)
- Some big-air takeoffs use a nose-press pre-rotation ("a full-press onto the nose of the board during that pre-rotation with the tail of the board completely off the snow") before popping — [Snowboard International, How big air works at the Olympics](https://www.snowboard.international/features/how-does-snowboard-big-air-work-at-the-winter-olympics/) (snippet, practitioner)
- Red Bull: "if you turn your shoulders, your board will turn with them" (keep the upper body steady on the approach) — [Red Bull, Snowboard jumps trick tips](https://www.redbull.com/us-en/snowboard-jumps-trick-tips) (snippet)

### Inferences (ESTIMATES)
- At 18 m/s, a 1 g carve needs R ≈ 33 m. Common sidecut radii are about 7–10 m (general knowledge, not sourced here), so a carved arc of R ≈ 33 m needs a fairly low edge angle. A clean carve on a long, gentle arc is therefore feasible, with no need to skid. A tight set-up arc (R < 20 m) at big-air speed would exceed 1.6 g and would in practice be skidded, costing speed. So model big-air set-ups as **carved, large-radius, small heading change (≈10°)**, with **"pronounced" set-up expressed through counter-rotated shoulders** instead of a sharper line.
- For a game that only lets the player speed-check (no steering), an automatic set-up of 2 shallow arcs works with these targets: peak lateral offset ≈ 1–2 m (≈0.3–0.5 × kicker width), R ≈ 15–20 m (park) or 30–50 m (big air), ending aligned and centred at the lip. The parameters fall straight out of v and a lateral-g budget (≈0.6–1.0 g).
